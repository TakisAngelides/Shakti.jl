Model equations

This page states the continuum model that Shakti.jl solves. The sheet is Shakti's (Sommers et al., 2018), extended to cavities that are partly empty and to ice that is lifted off the bed, as in GlaDS-2 (Wells et al., 2026) and (Schoof et al., 2012). Each extension can be turned off (see the tutorial), and the equations then reduce to those of Shakti. The Discretization page shows how the equations are solved on the grid.

Unknowns and geometry

The model is two-dimensional in the horizontal coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x, y)$. Between the bed $z_b(\mathbf{x})$ and the ice base there is a gap of height $b(\mathbf{x}, t)$, which holds a depth $W(\mathbf{x}, t)$ of water per unit bed area. The unknowns are

SymbolMeaningUnitState field
$b$gap height (space between the bed and the ice base)mb
$W$water depth (water volume per unit bed area)mW
$h$hydraulic head, referenced to the bedmh

and the derived fields are

\[H = \max\!\left(0,\; z_s - z_b - b - (W - b)_+\right), \qquad p_o = \rho_i g H, \qquad N = p_o - p_w ,\]

where $z_s$ is the ice surface, $H$ the ice thickness above the gap and the lifted water, $p_o$ the overburden pressure, $p_w$ the water pressure, $N$ the effective pressure and $(x)_+ = \max(x, 0)$. It is useful to write the overburden as a head, $h_o = z_b + p_o / (\rho_w g)$.

Three regimes of the sheet

The head and the water pressure are related by

\[p_w = \rho_w g \,\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(h - z_b,\; 0,\; \frac{p_o}{\rho_w g}\right), \qquad W = b + (h - z_b) - \frac{p_w}{\rho_w g} .\]

This one closure covers three regimes:

RegimeHeadPressureWater
unfilled (cavity partly empty)$h < z_b$$p_w = 0$, $N = p_o$$W = b - (z_b - h) < b$
filled$z_b \le h \le h_o$$p_w = \rho_w g (h - z_b)$$W = b$
uplifted (ice lifted off the bed)$h > h_o$$p_w = p_o$, $N = 0$$W = b + (h - h_o) > b$

So the bounds $0 \le p_w \le p_o$ hold, and once $p_w$ reaches a bound, changes in $h$ are changes in the water depth. FilledCavities() removes the lower bound and NoUplift() the upper one. With both, Shakti's sheet is recovered: $W = b$ and $p_w = \rho_w g (h - z_b)$ without bounds, so $N < 0$ (water above the overburden) and $p_w < 0$ (suction) are both possible.

Water flux

The water moves as a thin viscous layer driven by the gradient of the potential

\[\phi = h + b \quad (\texttt{SheetTopPotential}), \qquad \phi = h \quad (\texttt{BedPotential}),\]

the head of the top of the water, in metres. In the unfilled regime $\phi = z_b + W$ and in the uplifted regime $\phi = h_o + W$. $\rho_w g \phi$ is the potential of (Wells et al., 2026) Eq. 7. BedPotential is the potential of (Sommers et al., 2018), which leaves out the thickness of the water layer. The flux per unit width is

\[\mathbf{q} = -K \nabla \phi, \qquad K = \frac{g\, W^3}{12 \nu \left(1 + \omega \,\mathrm{Re}\right)}, \qquad \mathrm{Re} = \frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{\nu} .\]

At small Reynolds numbers this is laminar Poiseuille flow between parallel plates. As $\mathrm{Re}$ grows, the factor $1 + \omega \mathrm{Re}$ takes the flux to the turbulent limit $|\mathbf{q}| \propto |\nabla\phi|^{1/2}$. So the same equations describe distributed and channelized drainage, without a separate channel network (Sommers et al., 2018). The transmissivity is built from the water depth $W$, which equals $b$ in a filled gap, so an empty cavity carries no water. Since $\mathrm{Re}$ depends on $\mathbf{q}$, the two relations above make an implicit equation for $|\mathbf{q}|$. Writing $D = g W^3 |\nabla\phi| / (12 \nu^2)$, it is the quadratic $\omega \mathrm{Re}^2 + \mathrm{Re} - D = 0$, whose positive root is

\[\mathrm{Re} = \frac{2 D}{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 \omega D}} .\]

Water mass balance

The water per unit bed area changes by divergence of the flux, melt and input from above:

\[\frac{\partial W}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{q} = \frac{\dot m}{\rho_w} + i_{eb} .\]

$\dot m$ [kg m⁻² s⁻¹] is the melt rate of the ice at the base and $i_{eb}$ [m s⁻¹] the water that enters the bed from above (surface melt through moulins and crevasses, ieb). $E$ is the water stored in the ice above the bed, $E = e_v (h - z_b)$ with the englacial void ratio $e_v$ ((Sommers et al., 2018) Eq. 13, e_v = 0 by default). Where the unfilled and uplifted regimes are allowed, $E$ takes $h - z_b$ clamped to $[0, p_o/(\rho_w g)]$: outside that range the water is in $W$.

Gap height

The gap opens by melting of the ice roof and by sliding over bed bumps, and it closes by viscous creep of the ice:

\[\frac{\partial b}{\partial t} = \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + \beta(b)\, |\mathbf{u}_b| - A |N|^{n-1} N \, l_c(b) \;\left[ + \nabla \cdot (\mathcal{D} \nabla b) \right] .\]

$\mathbf{u}_b$ is the sliding velocity, $A$ the ice softness (A_visc) and $n$ the exponent of Glen's law. The opening by sliding is that of a cavity in the lee of bumps of height $b_r$ and spacing $l_r$,

\[\beta(b) = \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{b_r^* - b}{l_r}\right), \qquad b_r^* = \max\!\left(0,\; b_r - (W - b)_+\right),\]

where $b_r^*$ is the height of the bumps still in contact with the ice: ice lifted by a layer $W - b$ rides over the bumps lower than that layer. Without uplift $b_r^* = b_r$. The creep length scale is $l_c = b$. With the cutoff of (Felden et al., 2023) Eq. 7 (b_c > 0) it is $l_c = b^2 / b_c$ for $b \le b_c$ and $N > 0$, which slows the closure of thin gaps. At $N < 0$ the creep term opens the gap.

The term in brackets is optional (WithDiffusion). It is the melting of channel walls by the heat dissipated in the channel, as in Suhmo (Felden et al., 2023) Eq. 10, with

\[\mathcal{D} = \frac{\min(W, b)}{\rho_i L} \left( Q_\mathrm{diss} + Q_\mathrm{sens} \right)_+ ,\]

where $Q_\mathrm{diss}$ and $Q_\mathrm{sens}$ are the two flow terms of the melt rate below.

Melt rate

The heat available at the bed melts the ice at the rate

\[L \dot m = G + \underbrace{|\mathbf{u}_b \cdot \boldsymbol{\tau}_b|}_{Q_b} \underbrace{- \rho_w g\, \mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla \phi}_{Q_\mathrm{diss}} + \underbrace{c_t c_w \rho_w\, \mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla p_w}_{Q_\mathrm{sens}} - q_T ,\]

with the geothermal flux $G$, the frictional heat $Q_b$ of sliding with basal shear stress $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$, the heat $Q_\mathrm{diss} = \rho_w g K |\nabla\phi|^2 \ge 0$ dissipated by the flow, the sensible heat $Q_\mathrm{sens}$ needed to keep the water at the pressure-melting point (which falls by $c_t$ per pascal), and the heat $q_T$ conducted into the ice. Water that flows towards lower pressure, $\mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla p_w < 0$, is warmed to its rising melting point, which takes heat from melting. Each term can be turned off (mdot_includes_* in ModelParameters). $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$ comes from a sliding law in $N$ and $\mathbf{u}_b$ (table in the tutorial) or from an ice-flow model. Freeze-on ($\dot m < 0$) is limited to the water the gap holds.

Head equation

Subtracting the gap equation from the mass balance eliminates the time derivative of the gap and gives an equation for the head. In a filled cell ($W = b$) it reads

\[-\nabla \cdot \left( K \nabla (h + b) \right) + e_v \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} = \dot m \left( \frac{1}{\rho_w} - \frac{1}{\rho_i} \right) - \beta |\mathbf{u}_b| + A |N|^{n-1} N \, l_c \left[ - \nabla \cdot (\mathcal{D} \nabla b) \right] + i_{eb} ,\]

with $N = p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)$. On the left is the outflow of water and its englacial storage; on the right is the water that the gap cannot take up: the melt water less the room the melt makes (negative, since $\rho_w > \rho_i$), less the room that sliding opens, plus the water that creep pushes out, plus the input. With $e_v = 0$ the equation is elliptic at each time and nonlinear in $h$, through $N$ in the creep term and through $\mathrm{Re}$ and $\dot m$ in $K$ and on the right. With $\phi = h$ it is the head equation of (Sommers et al., 2018). In an unfilled or uplifted cell, $N$ is fixed at $p_o$ or $0$ and the water depth takes up the balance instead: the $\partial W / \partial t$ that the gap does not carry stays on the left as a storage term in $h$ ($W - b = h - z_b$ or $h - h_o$). The head equation and the gap equation are coupled through $b$ in $K$ and through $N$ in the closure; Discretization shows how a time step solves them.

Boundary conditions

Every cell has a mask value. The equations above hold on GROUNDED cells. The other values set the boundary conditions:

MaskCondition
OCEAN$h = z_b + \left(p_\mathrm{atm} - \rho_{sw} g \min(z_b, 0)\right) / (\rho_w g)$: the pressure of the ocean at the bed
LAND$h = z_b + p_\mathrm{atm} / (\rho_w g)$: water leaves at atmospheric pressure
OTHER_BASIN, FROZEN_BEDno flux across the face; the head is held. On a frozen bed $p_w = 0$ and $N = p_o$
edge of the gridno flux

By default OCEAN and LAND faces are outflow only (outflow_only_land, outflow_only_ocean): the face closes when the boundary head is above the water level $\phi$ of the grounded cell, so the boundary takes water from the bed but does not feed it.
