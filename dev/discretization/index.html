Discretization

This page shows how the equations of Model equations are discretized. Space is discretized by finite volumes on a regular grid and time by backward Euler. Each step solves the head equation by a Picard iteration and then moves the gap. Unless said otherwise the defaults are described: the GlaDS-2 sheet, ConsistentGapCoupling, "implicit_upwind" faces and FullyImplicitGapScheme.

Grid

Grid(nx, ny, lx, ly) has $n_x \times n_y$ cells with centres $\mathbf{x}_{ij} = ((i-1)\Delta x, (j-1)\Delta y)$, $\Delta x = l_x / (n_x - 1)$, $\Delta y = l_y / (n_y - 1)$. The fields $b$, $W$, $h$, $p_w$, $N$ and $\dot m$ live at cell centres; $K$, $\mathrm{Re}$ and the flux $q$ live on faces (staggered, q_x of size $(n_x + 1) \times n_y$ and q_y of size $n_x \times (n_y + 1)$). The face formulas are written for the $x$ face $i - \tfrac12$ between cells $i - 1$ and $i$; the $y$ faces are the same with $\Delta y$.

Time step

Step $n \to n+1$ of length $\Delta t$ (step!):

  1. From the start-of-step gap $b^n$ and water $W^n$: the overburden $p_o$, the jumps of the gap across faces (for $\phi = h + b$), the opening coefficient $\beta$ and the freeze-on limit. These are held for the whole step. A coupled ice model may have changed $z_s$, $z_b$, $\mathbf{u}_b$ before this.
  2. Solve the head equation for $h^{n+1}$ by Picard iteration. Every field that depends on the head, $p_w$, $N$, $\mathbf{q}$, $\mathrm{Re}$, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$ and $\dot m$, is recomputed at every iterate, so at convergence they are evaluated at $n+1$.
  3. Move the gap, $b^{n+1} = \mathcal{G}(b^n; N^{n+1}, \dot m^{n+1})$, with the gap scheme $\mathcal{G}$.
  4. Set the water $W^{n+1}$ and refresh $\beta$ and $l_c$ from $b^{n+1}$ for the next step.

The head equation contains $\partial b / \partial t$, which step 3 has not computed yet. The gap coupling decides what step 2 assumes for it. Under ConsistentGapCoupling the head rows assume exactly $\mathcal{G}$, so steps 2 and 3 store the same water and the water budget closes at any $\Delta t$. Under ExplicitGapCoupling (Shakti) they assume $b^{n+1} - b^n = \Delta t\, \dot b(N^{n+1}, b^n)$, which differs from $\mathcal{G}$ where creep is fast ($\Delta t A |N|^n > 1$), and the difference is created water (Gap coupling).

Face flux

On a face the driving gradient is the difference of the potential $\phi = h + b$, with the gap held at $b^n$:

\[(\nabla\phi)_{i-\frac12} = \frac{(h_i + b^n_i) - (h_{i-1} + b^n_{i-1})}{\Delta x} ,\]

and without the $b^n$ terms under BedPotential. At a face to an OCEAN or LAND cell the boundary side's gap counts as 0, since its fixed head is already the level of its water. The laminar conductance of the face is

\[K_{0,f} = \frac{g}{12\nu} \, \overline{W^3}_f ,\]

where $\overline{W^3}_f$ is set by k_face_choice: the value of the donor cell (the cell with the higher $\phi$, the one the water flows out of) for "implicit_upwind" and "upwind"; the arithmetic or harmonic mean of the two cells for "arithmetic" and "harmonic". Taking the donor's depth is what keeps an empty cell from draining water it does not have. With $D_f = K_{0,f} |(\nabla\phi)_f| / \nu$, the Reynolds number is the root of the quadratic of the flux law, and the face flux follows from it:

\[\mathrm{Re}_f = \frac{2 D_f}{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 \omega D_f}}, \qquad K_f = \frac{K_{0,f}}{1 + \omega \mathrm{Re}_f}, \qquad q_f = -K_f (\nabla\phi)_f .\]

This form avoids cancellation as $\omega \to 0$ and gives $|q_f| = \nu \mathrm{Re}_f$ exactly, with no lag between $q$ and $\mathrm{Re}$. The same $K_f$ is assembled into the matrix and used for every diagnostic ($\mathbf{q}$, $\mathrm{Re}$, $\dot m$, $\mathcal{D}$), so the water that the solve conserves and the water that melts the ice are the same. A face to an OCEAN or LAND cell takes the grounded cell's $W$, and a face to OTHER_BASIN, FROZEN_BED or the edge of the grid has $K_f = 0$.

The discrete head equation

For cell $P$ with neighbours $F$ across its four faces $f$, the divergence of the face fluxes is

\[(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q})_P = \frac{q_{i+\frac12} - q_{i-\frac12}}{\Delta x} + \frac{q_{j+\frac12} - q_{j-\frac12}}{\Delta y} ,\]

and integrating the mass balance over the cell over the step gives the row of cell $P$:

\[\sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( h_P - h_F \right) + \frac{E(h_P) - E(h_P^n)}{\Delta t} = \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{eb,P} - \frac{\mathcal{W}_P(h_P) - W^n_P}{\Delta t} - \sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( b^n_P - b^n_F \right) ,\]

where $\Delta_f$ is $\Delta x$ or $\Delta y$ and $\mathcal{W}_P(h)$ is the water that the cell holds at the end of the step at head $h$. By the closure of Three regimes of the sheet

\[\mathcal{W}_P(h) = \begin{cases} \mathcal{G}_P(p_o) - (z_b - h) & h < z_b \quad \text{(unfilled)} \\ \mathcal{G}_P\!\left(p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)\right) & z_b \le h \le h_o \quad \text{(filled)} \\ \mathcal{G}_P(0) + (h - h_o) & h > h_o \quad \text{(uplifted)} \end{cases}\]

with $\mathcal{G}_P(N)$ the gap after the step at effective pressure $N$. The three branches agree at $h = z_b$ and $h = h_o$, so a cell fills, pressurizes and lifts the ice continuously. In the unfilled and uplifted branches $\mathcal{W}_P$ grows with $h$ with slope 1, which puts $1/\Delta t$ on the diagonal; in the filled branch the slope is $\rho_w g\, |\partial \mathcal{G}_P / \partial N|$ (closure grows with $N$), computed by a central difference. The englacial storage $E(h) = e_v (h - z_b)$ adds $e_v / \Delta t$ to the diagonal.

Under ExplicitGapCoupling, in the filled branch, $(\mathcal{W}_P - W^n_P)/\Delta t$ is replaced by $(b^n_P - W^n_P)/\Delta t$ (zero in a full gap) plus the gap rate at $b^n$,

\[\frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_i} + \beta(b^n_P)\, |\mathbf{u}_b|_P - A_P |N_P|^{n-1} N_P\, l_c(b^n_P) ,\]

which is the right-hand side of Head equation on the grid, the scheme of (Sommers et al., 2018).

A cell whose four faces are all closed has no neighbour to exchange water with. Its row is solved exactly: the water at the end of the step, $W^n + \Delta t (\dot m / \rho_w + i_{eb})$, is known, and $\mathcal{W}_P(h) =$ that water is solved for $h$ by bisection. An OCEAN or LAND neighbour's head is known, so it is moved to the right-hand side, which keeps the matrix symmetric where the face scheme allows it. OCEAN, LAND, OTHER_BASIN and FROZEN_BED cells have the row $h_P =$ their boundary head (or, for the last two, their current head).

Picard iteration

The row is nonlinear in $h$ through $N$ in $\mathcal{G}$, through $W$ and $\mathrm{Re}$ in $K_f$, and through $\mathbf{q}$ and $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b(N)$ in $\dot m$. Iterate $k \to k+1$ (PicardSolver):

  1. Evaluate $K_f$, $\dot m$ and the regime of every cell at $h^k$, and linearize $\mathcal{W}_P$ about $h^k_P$: $\mathcal{W}_P(h) \approx \mathcal{W}_P(h^k) + \mathcal{W}_P'(h^k)\,(h - h^k)$. Under ExplicitGapCoupling the creep term is linearized the same way, which puts $n \rho_w g A |N|^{n-1} l_c$ on the diagonal.
  2. Under "implicit_upwind", $K_f$ takes the donor's water depth at the end of the step, $\mathcal{W}_d(h^k)$, and the row also takes the Newton term of the face flux in the donor's head, $\partial |q_f| / \partial h_d = 3 K_f |(\nabla\phi)_f|\, (1 + \omega\mathrm{Re}_f) / \left(W_d (1 + 2\omega\mathrm{Re}_f)\right) \cdot \mathcal{W}_d'$. The iteration is then Newton's method in the water depth, which converges at any $\Delta t$, where a lagged depth ("upwind") would be stable only for $\Delta t < \Delta x\, W / (3 |q|)$. The matrix keeps the sign pattern of an M-matrix but is not symmetric.
  3. Solve the sparse five-point system for $h^{k+1}$ with the linear solver (the tutorial).
  4. Limit the update where it crosses a kink of the row (the bed, the overburden head, the head of an outflow-only face) so that each solve sees the regime it ends in, and halve an update that reverses the previous one. Optionally relax, $h^{k+1} \leftarrow \alpha h^{k+1} + (1 - \alpha) h^k$.
  5. Stop when $\max |h^{k+1} - h^k| / \max |h^{k+1}| < \mathrm{tol}$ (or $< \mathrm{atol}$). Where the rows store $\mathcal{W}_P$ exactly (the defaults) it also has to hold that every cell's water $\mathcal{W}_P(h^{k+1})$ matches what its fluxes and sources leave: a limited update can stall the head without solving the row.

The first iterate is the head extrapolated in time from the last converged steps (head_extrapolation_order).

Gap update

FullyImplicitGapScheme evaluates the opening and the closure at $b^{n+1}$:

\[b^{n+1} = b^n + \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + \gamma \left(b_r^* - b^{n+1}\right)_+ - C\, b^{n+1} \right), \qquad \gamma = \frac{|\mathbf{u}_b|}{l_r}, \quad C = A |N|^{n-1} N ,\]

at $N = N^{n+1}$ and $\dot m = \dot m^{n+1}$. It is piecewise linear in $b^{n+1}$, so it is solved in closed form:

\[b^{n+1} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{b^n + \Delta t\, (\dot m / \rho_i + \gamma b_r^*)}{1 + \Delta t\, (\gamma + C)} & \text{if this is } < b_r^* , \\[2ex] \dfrac{b^n + \Delta t\, \dot m / \rho_i}{1 + \Delta t\, C} & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

Each branch is the step of a linear equation $\dot b = S - R b$. When its rate $R$ is negative ($N < 0$, where creep opens the gap) the update is the exact solution over the step, $b^n e^{|R|\Delta t} + S (e^{|R|\Delta t} - 1)/|R|$, instead of backward Euler, which would divide by $1 - \Delta t |R|$. With the cutoff $l_c = b^2/b_c$ the branches below $b_c$ are quadratics, solved the same way. The result is clamped to $[b_\min, b_\max]$. The update holds for any $\Delta t$. ExplicitGapScheme is forward Euler, $b^{n+1} = b^n + \Delta t\, \dot b(N^{n+1}, b^n)$, stable only for $\Delta t\, |\mathbf{u}_b| / l_r < 1$. Under WithDiffusion the term $\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{D}\nabla b)$ couples the cells, and the gap is solved as a second linear system, with $\mathcal{D}$ on the faces averaged from the cells.

Water and the budget

Under UnfilledCavities the water is advanced with the converged fluxes,

\[W^{n+1}_P = \max\!\left(0,\; W^n_P + \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{eb,P} - (\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q})_P \right) - \left( E(h^{n+1}_P) - E(h^n_P) \right) \right),\]

so the empty part of the gap is $b^{n+1} - W^{n+1}$ and any water above the gap is the uplift layer. Otherwise $W^{n+1} = \mathcal{W}_P(h^{n+1})$. water_created(sim) sums, over the grounded cells, the water the step leaves beyond its sources and fluxes. Under ConsistentGapCoupling it is zero to the tolerance of the Picard iteration.

Melt on the grid

Each term of $\dot m$ in a cell is built from its four faces. The flow terms average the face products over the two faces in each direction,

\[Q_{\mathrm{diss},P} = \rho_w g \left| \sum_f \tfrac12\, q_f (\nabla\phi)_f \right|, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{sens},P} = c_t c_w \rho_w \sum_f \tfrac12\, q_f (\nabla p_w)_f ,\]

except that a face to an OCEAN or LAND cell is credited in full to the grounded cell, so the heat of the outflow is not lost. $Q_b$ averages $u_b \tau_b$ from the faces in the same way, with weight $\tfrac12$. With limit_freeze_on (default) the melt rate is floored at the freeze-on the gap can supply in the step, $\dot m \ge -\rho_i \max\!\left(0, (b^n - b_\min)/\Delta t + \beta |\mathbf{u}_b| - C\, l_c\right)$, with each term evaluated where the gap scheme evaluates it, and under UnfilledCavities at most the water $W^n$ the cell holds.
