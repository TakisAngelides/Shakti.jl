Discretization

This page shows how the equations of Model equations are solved on the grid. Space is discretized by finite volumes on a regular grid, and time by backward Euler. Each time step first solves the head equation by a Picard iteration and then moves the gap. Unless said otherwise, the page describes the defaults, which are GlaDS2Sheet(), ConsistentGapCoupling, "implicit_upwind" faces and FullyImplicitGapScheme. The safeguards that make the iteration robust, and the bookkeeping around the solve, are in Further numerical details.

Grid

Grid(nx, ny, lx, ly) has $n_x \times n_y$ cells with centres $\mathbf{x}_{ij} = ((i-1)\Delta x, (j-1)\Delta y)$, where $\Delta x = l_x / (n_x - 1)$ and $\Delta y = l_y / (n_y - 1)$, so $l_x$ and $l_y$ are the distances between the outermost centres. The fields $b$, $W$, $h$, $p_w$, $N$ and $\dot m$ live at the cell centres. The transmissivity $K$, the Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}$ and the flux $q$ live on the faces between them (a staggered grid, where q_x has size $(n_x + 1) \times n_y$ and q_y has size $n_x \times (n_y + 1)$). The face formulas below are written for the $x$ face $i - \tfrac12$ between cells $i - 1$ and $i$. The $y$ faces are the same with $\Delta y$.

Time step

Integrating the Water mass balance over a cell $P$ and dividing by its area gives one ordinary differential equation per cell for its total water thickness,

\[\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\left( W_P + b_{e,P} \right) + \sum_f \frac{\sigma_f\, q_f}{\Delta_f} = \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} ,\]

where the sum runs over the four faces of the cell, $\sigma_f = +1$ on its east and north faces and $-1$ on its west and south faces, and $\Delta_f$ is $\Delta x$ or $\Delta y$. Here $W_P$ and $b_{e,P}$ are averages over the cell. The cell's edges are fixed, so the averages depend on time only, and the time derivative becomes an ordinary one, $\mathrm d/\mathrm dt$. With the two-point flux of Face flux, every face term takes the same form, $\sigma_f q_f / \Delta_f = K_f (\hat{h}_P - \hat{h}_F) / \Delta_f^2$, with $F$ the neighbour across face $f$. Integrating from $t^{(m)}$ to $t^{(m+1)} = t^{(m)} + \Delta t$, the derivative gives the change of the total water thickness over the step exactly. Backward Euler approximates the other time integrals by $\Delta t$ times their integrand at the end of the step, so that

\[\frac{W_P^{(m+1)} - W_P^{(m)}}{\Delta t} + \frac{b_{e,P}^{(m+1)} - b_{e,P}^{(m)}}{\Delta t} + \sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( \hat{h}_P - \hat{h}_F \right) = \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} ,\]

with $K_f$, $\hat{h}$, $\dot m$ and $i_{e\to b}$ at $t^{(m+1)}$. The scheme is first-order accurate in time. In space the two-point difference is second-order accurate, and the accuracy of $K_f$ depends on the face rule. $W^{(m)}_P$ and $b^{(m)}_{e,P}$ are the water and the englacial water that the previous step ended with. A few quantities are held at their start-of-step values instead of at $t^{(m+1)}$, as listed below.

A step $m \to m+1$ of length $\Delta t$ (step!) does four things.

  1. From the gap $b^{(m)}$ and the water $W^{(m)}$ at the start of the step it computes the overburden $p_o$, the jumps of the gap across the faces (for $\hat{h} = h + b$) and the freeze-on limit. These, and the opening coefficient $\beta$ and creep length $l_c$ left by the previous step, are held for the whole step. A coupled ice model may have changed $z_s$, $z_b$ and $\mathbf{u}_b$ before this.
  2. The head equation is solved for $h^{(m+1)}$ by Picard iteration. Every field that depends on the head ($p_w$, $N$, $\mathbf{q}$, $\mathrm{Re}$, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$ and $\dot m$) is recomputed at every iterate, so at convergence all of them are evaluated at $t^{(m+1)}$.
  3. The gap scheme $\mathcal{G}$ moves the gap, $b^{(m+1)} = \mathcal{G}(b^{(m)}; N^{(m+1)}, \dot m^{(m+1)})$.
  4. The step sets $W^{(m+1)}$ and refreshes $\beta$ and $l_c$ from $b^{(m+1)}$ for the next step.

The head equation contains $\partial b / \partial t$, which step 3 has not computed yet, so step 2 must assume a value for it. The gap coupling decides which. Under ConsistentGapCoupling the head rows assume exactly $\mathcal{G}$, so steps 2 and 3 store the same water and the water budget closes at any $\Delta t$. Under ExplicitGapCoupling (Shakti's original scheme) they assume $b^{(m+1)} - b^{(m)} = \Delta t\, \dot b(N^{(m+1)}, b^{(m)})$. This differs from $\mathcal{G}$ where creep is fast ($\Delta t A |N|^n > 1$), and the difference is water created or lost (Gap coupling).

Face flux

The flux across a face is driven by the difference of the hydraulic head $\hat{h} = h + b$ between its two cells, with the gap held at $b^{(m)}$:

\[(\nabla\hat{h})_{i-\frac12} = \frac{(h_i + b^{(m)}_i) - (h_{i-1} + b^{(m)}_{i-1})}{\Delta x} .\]

Under BedPotential the $b^{(m)}$ terms are dropped. On a face to an OCEAN or LAND cell the boundary side's gap counts as 0, since its fixed head is already the level of its water.

The laminar transmissivity of the face is

\[K_{0,f} = \frac{g}{12\nu} \, \overline{W^3}_f ,\]

where $\overline{W^3}_f$ combines the two cells' water thicknesses as k_face_choice says:

k_face_choice$\overline{W^3}_f$
"implicit_upwind" (default on the free-surface sheets)the donor's $W^3$, with $W$ the donor's water at the end of the step
"upwind"the donor's $W^3$, with $W$ the donor's water at the start of the step
"arithmetic" (default on the filled sheet)$(W_{i-1}^3 + W_i^3)/2$
"harmonic"$2 W_{i-1}^3 W_i^3 / (W_{i-1}^3 + W_i^3)$
"gap_mean"$\left((W_{i-1} + W_i)/2\right)^3$, the face rule of SUHMO

The donor is the cell with the higher $\hat{h}$, the one the water flows out of. Taking the donor's water thickness keeps an empty cell from draining water it does not have, which is why the averaged faces are refused on the free-surface sheets. Within a step, a face whose donor has reversed four times keeps its donor, so the iteration cannot cycle on it. A face to an OCEAN or LAND cell takes the grounded cell's $W$, and a face to OTHER_BASIN, FROZEN_BED or the edge of the grid has $K_f = 0$.

With the free-surface layer on (the default), each cell's $W^3$ is first multiplied by $1 + (f_K - 1)\, w$, its weight $w$ taken at the head iterate, and the face's $\omega$ becomes $\omega_f = \omega\,(1 + (f_{\omega} - 1)\, w_f)$. $w_f$ is the weight of the cell whose transmissivity the face takes (the donor, or the grounded cell at a boundary face), or the mean of the two cells' weights for a mean of their transmissivities. Otherwise $\omega_f = \omega$.

The turbulent correction follows from the flux law. With $D_f = K_{0,f} |(\nabla\hat{h})_f| / \nu$, the Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re} = |q|/\nu$ solves $\omega_f \mathrm{Re}^2 + \mathrm{Re} - D_f = 0$, so

\[\mathrm{Re}_f = \frac{2 D_f}{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 \omega_f D_f}}, \qquad K_f = \frac{K_{0,f}}{1 + \omega_f \mathrm{Re}_f}, \qquad q_f = -K_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f .\]

This form of the root avoids cancellation as $\omega \to 0$ and gives $|q_f| = \nu \mathrm{Re}_f$ exactly, with no lag between $q$ and $\mathrm{Re}$. The same $K_f$ is assembled into the matrix and used by every diagnostic ($\mathbf{q}$, $\mathrm{Re}$, $\dot m$, $\mathcal{D}$), so the water that the solve conserves and the water that melts the ice are the same.

Boundary cells and faces

A LAND or OCEAN cell has a fixed reduced head $h_D$ at its centre, with the value of Boundary conditions. Since the head sits at the cell centre and not on the face, a face to land or ocean spans the same distance $\Delta_f$ between two cell centres as an interior face. The boundary cell holds no gap, $b = 0$, so its hydraulic head is $h_D$ itself, and the gap jump across the face is the grounded cell's gap, $\pm b^{(m)}_P$.

The boundary faces are outflow-only by default (outflow_only_land, outflow_only_ocean), and ShaktiSheet(original = true) keeps them open in both directions. The flux out of a grounded cell $P$ through such a face is

\[q_f = \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f} \max\left(0,\; h_P + b^{(m)}_P - h_D\right) ,\]

so the face is closed, $K_f = 0$, while the boundary head lies above the grounded cell's water level $h + b$, and the boundary drains the ice but never feeds it. As a function of $h_P$ the flux is zero below $h_D - b^{(m)}_P$ and linear above, which puts a kink in the row of $P$. Whether each such face is open is therefore decided again from the head at every Picard iterate, and an update that crosses the kink stops at it (Picard iteration, step 4).

With the option off, the face is an ordinary open face. Its flux $K_f (h_P + b^{(m)}_P - h_D) / \Delta_f$ may then be negative, and water enters the bed from the boundary, for example seawater beneath grounded ice.

The discrete head equation

For a cell $P$ with neighbours $F$ across its four faces $f$, the divergence of the face fluxes is

\[(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q})_P = \frac{q_{i+\frac12} - q_{i-\frac12}}{\Delta x} + \frac{q_{j+\frac12} - q_{j-\frac12}}{\Delta y} .\]

Integrating the mass balance over the cell and over the step gives the row of cell $P$:

\[\sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( h_P - h_F \right) + \frac{b_e(h_P) - b^{(m)}_{e,P}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} - \frac{\mathcal{W}_P(h_P) - W^{(m)}_P}{\Delta t} - \sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( b^{(m)}_P - b^{(m)}_F \right) ,\]

where $\Delta_f$ is $\Delta x$ or $\Delta y$. On the left are the net outflow and the englacial storage. On the right are the sources, the change of the water at the bed, and the part of the outflow driven by the known gap jumps. $\mathcal{W}_P(h)$, the water-thickness function, is the water at the bed, the water thickness $W_P$, at the end of the step if the head of the cell is $h$. The total water thickness of the cell is $\mathcal{W}_P(h) + b_e(h)$, the water at the bed together with the englacial water. By the closure of Three regimes of the sheet,

\[\mathcal{W}_P(h) = \begin{cases} \mathcal{G}_P(p_o) - (z_b - h) & h < z_b \quad \text{(unfilled)} \\ \mathcal{G}_P\!\left(p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)\right) & z_b \le h \le h_o \quad \text{(filled)} \\ \mathcal{G}_P(0) + (h - h_o) & h > h_o \quad \text{(uplifted)} \end{cases}\]

with $\mathcal{G}_P(N)$ the gap after the step at effective pressure $N$. The three regimes agree at $h = z_b$ and $h = h_o$, so a cell fills, pressurizes and lifts the ice continuously. In the unfilled and uplifted regimes $\mathcal{W}_P$ grows with $h$ with slope 1, which puts $1/\Delta t$ on the diagonal. In the filled regime the slope is $\rho_w g\, |\mathrm d\mathcal{G}_P / \mathrm dN|$ (closure grows with $N$), computed by a central difference. On the dry floor the filled regime moves the water $W^{(m)}$ instead of the gap, with no lower clamp at $b_{\min}$, so $\mathcal{W}_P$ keeps a non-zero slope in a drained cell.

Englacial storage

The englacial water $b_e = e_v\, p_w / (\rho_w g)$ is the water held in the ice above the bed, per unit area of bed. By default $e_v = 0$ and there is none. With unfilled cavities and uplift $p_w$ lies between 0 and $p_o$, so

\[b_e(h) = e_v \min\!\left( \max(h - z_b,\, 0),\; \frac{p_o}{\rho_w g} \right), \qquad \frac{\mathrm d b_e}{\mathrm d h} = \begin{cases} e_v & \text{filled} \\ 0 & \text{unfilled or uplifted} . \end{cases}\]

Below the bed and above the overburden head $b_e$ stays constant, and any further change of the head empties the gap or lifts the ice instead. Without unfilled cavities the lower limit is dropped, and without uplift the upper one.

The row stores the change over the step, $\big(b_e(h^{(m+1)}) - b^{(m)}_e\big)/\Delta t$, the backward-Euler step of $\partial b_e / \partial t$. Linearized about the iterate, it adds $(\mathrm d b_e/\mathrm d h)/\Delta t$ to the diagonal.

The start-of-step value $b^{(m)}_e$ is the value the last step ended with, kept in the State field b_e. It is not recomputed from $h^{(m)}$, because $p_o$ changes between steps with the gap and the surface. Recomputing it would change the englacial water of an uplifted cell by $e_v\, \Delta p_o / (\rho_w g)$ without any flux. With the stored value, the drop in englacial water appears in the englacial term of the row, $\big(b_e(h^{(m+1)}) - b^{(m)}_e\big)/\Delta t$. The row then moves that water into the sheet within the step, where the budget counts it, so no water is lost.

With $e_v > 0$ the total water thickness gradient of a filled cell, $\mathrm d(\mathcal{W} + b_e)/\mathrm dh$, is at least $e_v$, so the diagonal of every row stays positive even when all its faces are closed. Closed cells then take the ordinary row (Closed cells).

On the filled sheet the englacial storage also switches off the filled cap of Limiting the head iterate. Take one filled cell with a face of transmissivity $K_f$ to a neighbour at distance $\Delta x$. Without englacial storage, linearizing its water-thickness function $\mathcal{W}$ about the iterate and solving the row gives the step

\[h^{(k+1)} - h^{(k)} = -\frac{\mathcal{R}(h^{(k)})}{(\mathrm d\mathcal{W}/\mathrm dh)/\Delta t + K_f/\Delta x^2},\]

the imbalance $\mathcal{R}$ of the row divided by how strongly the row responds to the head. In a filled cell $\mathcal{W} = \mathcal{G}(N)$. On thick ice near $N = p_o$ creep closes the gap almost completely within the step, whatever the exact $N$, and a nearly closed gap can barely change. Under 1000 m of ice, with a time step of one day and no sliding, a gap that the update closes to 1 mm gives $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}/\mathrm dh \approx 3 \times 10^{-6}$, so where $K_f$ is small too, an imbalance of 10 mm of water over the step gives a step of about 3000 m of head. That is more than three times the 910 m between the bed and the overburden head, while the head that balances the water lies about 500 m away. As the head rises, $N$ falls, creep slows and $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}/\mathrm dh$ grows quickly, which the slope at the iterate does not see. The cap stops this overshoot. With $e_v > 0$ the gradient in the step is the total water thickness gradient $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}/\mathrm dh + e_v$, about 30 times larger at $e_v = 10^{-4}$. This straight part dominates where $\mathcal{W}$ is flat, so the step comes out of the right size, and the cap would only cut good steps short and slow the iteration down.

Gap couplings

The head rows are solved before the gap update has run (Time step), so each row must assume what the gap will be at the end of the step. The gap coupling decides which gap that is.

Under ConsistentGapCoupling (the default) the row assumes the gap that the gap update $\mathcal{G}_P$ itself will give. The row then stores the water-thickness function $\mathcal{W}_P(h)$ built from $\mathcal{G}_P$ (The discrete head equation). In the three regimes

regimewater-thickness function $\mathcal{W}_P(h)$gradient $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_P/\mathrm dh$
unfilled, $h < z_b$$\mathcal{G}_P(p_o) - (z_b - h)$1
filled$\mathcal{G}_P(N)$, $N = p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)$$\rho_w g \max\!\left(0,\, -\mathrm d\mathcal{G}_P / \mathrm dN\right)$

The unfilled and uplifted rows are linear in $h$. They evaluate $\mathcal{G}_P$ at $N = p_o$ and $N = 0$, which are the values of the filled row at $h = z_b$ and at $h = h_o$, so the rows join continuously. The filled row is nonlinear in $h$ and is linearized about the iterate, $\mathcal{W}_P(h) \approx \mathcal{G}_P(N^{(k)}) + (\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_P/\mathrm dh)(h - h^{(k)})$, with the gradient taken at $N^{(k)}$. That gradient is derived in Derivative of the gap update. The melt rate in $\mathcal{G}_P$ is recomputed from the fluxes at every iterate, so the row conserves the water of the melt at the converged head. Only the transmissivity holds the melt of the start of the step (Melt in the transmissivity). The transmissivity sets the size of a face flux, and both cells of the face take the same flux, so this lag changes the accuracy of the head but creates or loses no water.

Under ExplicitGapCoupling the row instead assumes that the gap changes at the rate it has for the start-of-step gap and the new effective pressure,

\[b^{(m+1)} - b^{(m)} = \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + \beta(b^{(m)})\, |\mathbf{u}_b| - C(N)\, l_c(b^{(m)}) \right), \qquad C(N) = A |N|^{n-1} N .\]

The change of the water thickness over the step, $(\mathcal{W}_P - W^{(m)}_P)/\Delta t$, then becomes

\[\frac{b^{(m)}_P - W^{(m)}_P}{\Delta t} + \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_i} + \beta(b^{(m)}_P)\, |\mathbf{u}_b|_P - C(N_P)\, l_c(b^{(m)}_P) ,\]

with $N_P = p_o - \rho_w g (h_P - z_b)$ in a filled cell. An unfilled cell has $N_P = p_o$ and adds $(h_P - z_b)/\Delta t$, the change of its empty part. An uplifted cell has $N_P = 0$ and adds $(h_P - h_o)/\Delta t$, the change of its uplift layer. The first term, $(b^{(m)}_P - W^{(m)}_P)/\Delta t$, is the empty part of the gap (positive) or the uplift layer (negative) that the last step left, which the row fills or drains first. It is zero in a cell that ended the last step filled. The filled row ignores the clamps of the gap update at $b_{\min}$ and $b_{\max}$. Where a clamp binds, the water the clamp keeps out of the gap is lost at $b_{\max}$ and created at $b_{\min}$. The unfilled and uplifted rows set the rate to zero where the gap is held at a clamp.

The creep term changes steeply with $N$, so it is linearized about the iterate (Newton). Since $\mathrm dC/\mathrm dh = -\rho_w g\, n A |N|^{n-1}$,

\[C(N)\, l_c \approx C(N^{(k)})\, l_c - g_d \left( h - h^{(k)} \right), \qquad g_d = n \rho_w g A |N^{(k)}|^{n-1} l_c ,\]

which puts $g_d$ on the diagonal and $C(N^{(k)})\, l_c + g_d h^{(k)}$ on the right-hand side. For $\Delta t R \ll 1$, $g_d$ is the limit of the gradient $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_P/\mathrm dh$ of the filled row of the consistent coupling (Derivative of the gap update), so the two couplings agree at short time steps.

Paired with FullyImplicitGapScheme, the explicit coupling no longer agrees with the gap update, and the step creates water. Take a cell whose gap stays in one case of the update over the step, with no clamp binding, and write that case as $\mathrm db/\mathrm dt = S - R\, b$ (Accuracy of the gap update). The row assumes that the gap changes by $\Delta t\, (S - R\, b^{(m)})$, while the update changes it by $\Delta t\, (S - R\, b^{(m)})/(1 + \Delta t R)$. The difference is water that the step creates (Water and the budget). It is positive where the gap closes, since the row sends away the full explicit closure but the update closes the gap less. For $\Delta t R \ll 1$ it is of order $\Delta t^2$ per step, the error of an operator split. For $\Delta t R \gg 1$ the change of the updated gap stays bounded, tending to $S/R - b^{(m)}$, while the change the row assumes grows with $\Delta t$. Nearly all of the closure the row assumed is then created water. Once $\Delta t R > 1 + \Delta t\, S / b^{(m)}$ the row even assumes that more water leaves the gap than it holds. Under ConsistentGapCoupling the rows and the update use the same $\mathcal{G}_P$, so no water is created this way at any $\Delta t$.

The explicit coupling is the scheme of the original Shakti model (Sommers et al., 2018). In a filled cell without englacial storage, with $W = b$ and with $h$ in place of $\hat{h}$ in the fluxes, the row is their head equation,

\[\sum_f \frac{K_f}{\Delta_f^2} \left( h_P - h_F \right) = \dot m_P \left( \frac{1}{\rho_w} - \frac{1}{\rho_i} \right) - \beta_P |\mathbf{u}_b|_P + A_P |N_P|^{n-1} N_P\, b^{(m)}_P + i_{e\to b,P} .\]

The original scheme then advances the gap with ExplicitGapScheme at the converged $N$, so its head equation and its gap update agree, but the gap update is stable only for $\Delta t\, R < 2$ (Accuracy of the gap update). ShaktiSheet(original = true) selects FilledCavities, NoUplift, BedPotential, ExplicitGapCoupling and boundary faces open in both directions. With ExplicitGapScheme and "arithmetic" faces added, Shakti.jl solves these equations with their time discretization, on a finite-volume grid in place of their finite elements. The original code solves for the head at the vertices of a triangular mesh and holds the gap, the Reynolds number and hence $K$ constant on each triangle. Two details still differ. Their transmissivity is constant on each triangle, built from the gap of that triangle, while ours is the mean of the two cells' laminar transmissivities on each face. Their Reynolds number is updated from the flux of the previous iterate, while ours solves the quadratic of Face flux on every face. Both give the same Reynolds number once the iteration has converged.

Special rows

Some rows are special:

  • A grounded cell whose four faces are all closed, with $e_v = 0$, exchanges no water. Its water at the end of the step, $W^{(m)} + \Delta t (\dot m / \rho_w + i_{e\to b})$, is known, and $\mathcal{W}_P(h)$ equal to it is solved for $h$ exactly, by bisection, or under ExplicitGapCoupling by inverting the creep law in closed form.
  • An OCEAN or LAND neighbour's head is known, so its term moves to the right-hand side. This keeps the matrix symmetric wherever the face scheme allows it. The face's transmissivity stays on the cell's diagonal, which makes the matrix positive definite next to an open boundary face.
  • OCEAN and LAND cells have the row $h_P =$ their boundary head, and OTHER_BASIN and FROZEN_BED cells the row $h_P =$ their current head.

Clamps on the effective pressure

$N$ can be clamped to $[N_{\min}, N_{\max}]$, for the whole grid (N_min and N_max of ModelParameters) or for single cells (CellNClamping, with its own bounds in each cell it lists). By default there is no clamp, $N_{\min} = -\infty$ and $N_{\max} = \infty$. The clamps are kept as a safeguard for the filled sheet.

From the head, every grounded or frozen-bed cell gets the water pressure $p_w = \rho_w g (h - z_b)$, floored at 0 with unfilled cavities and capped at $p_o$ with uplift. Its effective pressure is then

\[N = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(p_o - p_w,\; N_{\min},\; N_{\max}\right),\]

and CellNClamping clamps it again in its own cells. The head rows and the gap update both use this clamped $N$, while $p_w$ is not clamped. Where a clamp binds, $N \ne p_o - p_w$ and $N$ stays at the bound whatever the head of the cell. The gap the row assumes, and with it the water of the cell at the end of the step, then no longer changes with the head. The Newton term of the row, $(\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_P/\mathrm dh)/\Delta t$ or $g_d$ (Gap couplings), still appears on the diagonal and, times $h^{(k)}$, on the right-hand side. The two cancel once the iteration has converged, so the term only damps the iteration, and the head of the cell is set by its face fluxes.

With unfilled cavities or uplift, a clamp tighter than $[0, \infty)$ is refused when the simulation is set up. The unfilled row assumes $N = p_o$ and the uplifted row $N = 0$, while the gap update would close the gap at the clamped $N$. The difference would stay in $W$, and the $N$ passed to an ice-flow model would no longer be $p_o - p_w$.

Where a clamp binds, the water of the row no longer follows its head, so the water test of Picard iteration is used only where no clamp can bind. With unfilled cavities and uplift, $N$ already lies in $[0, p_o]$, so the clamps that remain allowed never bind and the test stays on. With unfilled cavities but no uplift, $p_w$ can exceed $p_o$ and $N$ can fall below 0, so even a floor at 0 can bind. Any finite floor then switches the water test off, whether it binds or not.

Picard iteration

The row of The discrete head equation is nonlinear in $h$. The head enters through $N$ in $\mathcal{G}$, through $W$ and $\mathrm{Re}$ in $K_f$, through $\mathbf{q}$ and $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b(N)$ in $\dot m$, and through the regime of each cell. The row is linearized about the current iterate $h^{(k)}$ in one of two ways. A coefficient that depends on $h$ can be evaluated at $h^{(k)}$ and held while the linear system is solved, which is a Picard, or fixed-point, iteration. A term $f(h)$ can instead be replaced by its tangent at $h^{(k)}$, $f(h^{(k)}) + (\mathrm df/\mathrm dh)(h^{(k)})\,(h - h^{(k)})$, which is Newton's method. The part $(\mathrm df/\mathrm dh)(h^{(k)})\, h$ that the tangent adds to the matrix is called a Newton term. Shakti.jl uses Newton terms for the water-thickness function $\mathcal{W}_P$ of each row and for the water thickness of the donor of each face, where the Picard iteration diverges at practical time steps (Why the water thickness is differentiated). It holds $\mathrm{Re}_f$, $\dot m$ and the regimes at $h^{(k)}$. The solver is called PicardSolver, but the iteration is a partial Newton method.

An iterate $k \to k+1$ does five things.

  1. Evaluate $K_f$, $\dot m$ and the regime of every cell at $h^{(k)}$. $\mathcal{W}_P$ has one formula in each regime, unfilled, filled and uplifted (Gap couplings). Each row takes the formula of the regime that $h^{(k)}_P$ lies in and keeps it for the solve, even if $h^{(k+1)}_P$ ends in another regime (step 4 keeps such crossings small). The regime is recomputed from the head at every iterate, so a cell that step 4 stopped just inside a new regime takes the formula of that regime in the next solve. The unfilled and uplifted formulas are already linear in $h$. The filled one is linearized about $h^{(k)}_P$, $\mathcal{W}_P(h) \approx \mathcal{W}_P(h^{(k)}) + (\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_P/\mathrm dh)(h^{(k)})\,(h - h^{(k)})$.
  2. Under "implicit_upwind", add the Newton terms of the donors' water thickness (Newton terms of the donors).
  3. Solve the sparse five-point system for $h^{(k+1)}$ with the linear solver (the tutorial).
  4. Damp and limit the change of the head. The optional under-relaxation and the stall damping scale the change first. The limits then act cell by cell, so that an update that crosses a kink of its row stops at the kink and the steps inside a regime stay bounded (Limiting the head iterate).
  5. Recompute $p_w$, $N$, the face fluxes and $\dot m$ at the new head, and stop when the tests of Convergence tests pass.

The terms held at $h^{(k)}$ do not change the head the iteration converges to. With $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}$ the residuals of the rows (left-hand side minus right-hand side) and $\mathbf{A}$ the matrix of an iterate, each linear system is built so that

\[\mathbf{A}\, \left(\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)} - \mathbf{h}^{(k)}\right) = -\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{h}^{(k)}) .\]

Once the head stops changing, the residuals are zero and the nonlinear rows are solved exactly. The held terms, the derivatives of $\mathrm{Re}_f$, $\dot m$ and the regimes, only make $\mathbf{A}$ differ from the Jacobian of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}$, its matrix of derivatives in $\mathbf{h}$. They affect how fast the iteration converges, not where it ends.

Newton terms of the donors

Under "implicit_upwind", $K_f$ takes the water thickness of the donor at the end of the step, $W_d = \mathcal{W}_d(h^{(k)})$, with its melt opening from the start of the step (Melt in the transmissivity). The outflow of the face then depends on the head of the donor through $W_d$. Holding $W_d$ at the iterate makes the iteration diverge at practical time steps (Why the water thickness is differentiated), so the outflow is linearized in the donor's head $h_d$ at a fixed head gradient,

\[|q_f|(h_d) \approx |q_f|(h^{(k)}_d) + J_f \left( h_d - h^{(k)}_d \right), \qquad J_f = \frac{\mathrm d|q_f|}{\mathrm dh_d} .\]

The row of the donor gains $J_f/\Delta_f$ on its diagonal, and the row of the receiver gains $-J_f/\Delta_f$ in the donor's column. The right-hand sides gain the same terms times $h^{(k)}_d$, so both cancel at $h = h^{(k)}$.

The gradient of the donor's water thickness in its head, $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d/\mathrm dh_d$, is the one of its own row (Gap couplings),

Where $\mathcal{W}_d$ would fall below 0, the water thickness and its gradient are both 0.

Without the free-surface layer the head of the donor changes only $W_d$. The flux is $|q_f| = \nu \mathrm{Re}_f$, with $\mathrm{Re}_f$ the root of $\omega \mathrm{Re}^2 + \mathrm{Re} - D_f = 0$, $D_f = K_{0,f} |(\nabla\hat{h})_f| / \nu$ and $K_{0,f} = g W_d^3/(12\nu)$ (Face flux). Differentiating the quadratic in $h_d$ at a fixed head gradient gives

\[\left( 1 + 2\omega\mathrm{Re}_f \right) \frac{\mathrm d\mathrm{Re}_f}{\mathrm dh_d} = \frac{\mathrm dD_f}{\mathrm dh_d} = \frac{3 D_f}{W_d}\, \frac{\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d}{\mathrm dh_d} .\]

With $\nu D_f = K_{0,f} |(\nabla\hat{h})_f| = (1 + \omega\mathrm{Re}_f)\, |q_f|$, multiplying by $\nu$ gives

\[J_f = \frac{3 |q_f| \, (1 + \omega\mathrm{Re}_f)}{W_d\, (1 + 2\omega\mathrm{Re}_f)}\, \frac{\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d}{\mathrm dh_d} \ge 0 ,\]

which is $3|q_f|/W_d$ times $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d/\mathrm dh_d$ in laminar flow and half of that in fully turbulent flow.

With the free-surface layer the head of the donor also changes its weight $w_d$. The weight enters the flux law twice (Face flux), in the laminar transmissivity and in the turbulent coefficient,

\[K_{0,f} = \frac{g}{12\nu} \left( 1 + a\, w_d \right) W_d^3, \qquad \omega_f = \omega \left( 1 + o\, w_d \right), \qquad a = f_K - 1, \quad o = f_{\omega} - 1 .\]

Inside the ramp of The free-surface switch the weight is $w = t^2 (3 - 2t)$ with $t = (z_b - h)/(\beta_{\mathrm{fs}}\, b)$. As the head rises the cell becomes less empty, $t$ falls and the weight falls with it,

\[\frac{\mathrm dw_d}{\mathrm dh_d} = -\frac{6\, t\, (1 - t)}{\beta_{\mathrm{fs}}\, b} \le 0 ,\]

and $\mathrm dw_d/\mathrm dh_d = 0$ outside the ramp. Differentiating $\omega_f \mathrm{Re}^2 + \mathrm{Re} - D_f = 0$ with both $D_f$ and $\omega_f$ changing gives

\[\left( 1 + 2\omega_f\mathrm{Re}_f \right) \frac{\mathrm d\mathrm{Re}_f}{\mathrm dh_d} = \frac{\mathrm dD_f}{\mathrm dh_d} - \mathrm{Re}_f^2 \frac{\mathrm d\omega_f}{\mathrm dh_d}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm dD_f}{\mathrm dh_d} = D_f \left( \frac{3}{W_d} \frac{\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d}{\mathrm dh_d} + \frac{a}{1 + a w_d} \frac{\mathrm dw_d}{\mathrm dh_d} \right), \qquad \frac{\mathrm d\omega_f}{\mathrm dh_d} = \omega\, o\, \frac{\mathrm dw_d}{\mathrm dh_d} .\]

Multiplying by $\nu$, with $\nu D_f = K_f (1 + \omega_f\mathrm{Re}_f) |(\nabla\hat{h})_f|$,

\[J_f = \frac{1}{1 + 2\omega_f\mathrm{Re}_f} \left[ K_f |(\nabla\hat{h})_f| (1 + \omega_f\mathrm{Re}_f) \left( \frac{3}{W_d} \frac{\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d}{\mathrm dh_d} + \frac{a}{1 + a w_d} \frac{\mathrm dw_d}{\mathrm dh_d} \right) - \nu\, \omega\, o\, \mathrm{Re}_f^2\, \frac{\mathrm dw_d}{\mathrm dh_d} \right] .\]

As the head rises, the first term in the round brackets is positive, since thicker water carries more. The second is negative, since the cell turns from a free-surface layer into a duct, which carries up to $f_K$ times less at the same water thickness. The last term is positive, since $\omega_f$ falls towards $\omega$ and turbulence resists the flow less. Where the second term outweighs the others, the outflow falls as the head rises and $J_f$ is negative. A negative $J_f$ shrinks the donor's diagonal and raises the entry of the donor in the receiver's row, which is $-K_f/\Delta_f^2 - J_f/\Delta_f$, and once $J_f < -K_f/\Delta_f$ that entry is positive. The receiver's head would then fall as the donor's rises, and the matrix would lose the M-matrix pattern below, on which the absence of spurious oscillations and the linear solvers rely. A negative $J_f$ is therefore set to 0. The face then keeps its transmissivity at $W_d(h^{(k)})$ for the solve, a Picard step for this one dependence, which can slow the iteration but does not change the head it converges to. A ramp at least as wide as the bound of The free-surface switch, which the default fs_blend = 0.65 is, keeps $J_f \ge 0$.

$J_f$ is also 0 where the face carries no flux, where the donor is not grounded, where $\mathrm d\mathcal{W}_d/\mathrm dh_d = 0$, and where the donor is held on the downstream side of the face (Donors near a flow divide). With $J_f \ge 0$ the matrix keeps the sign pattern of an M-matrix, a positive diagonal and no positive entries off it, but it is no longer symmetric (Linear solvers).

Convergence tests

The iteration stops when two tests pass, one on the head and one on the water. The head test asks that the head has stopped changing,

\[\frac{\max_P |h^{(k+1)}_P - h^{(k)}_P|}{\max_P |h^{(k+1)}_P|} < \epsilon_h ,\]

with $\epsilon_h$ = tol ($10^{-6}$ by default). The change is the one applied after the limits, divided by the factors of the stall damping and of the optional under-relaxation, so that a damped iteration does not stop early. With Anderson acceleration the test uses the accelerator's own residual instead.

The head test alone is not enough, for two reasons. Relative to the largest head, $10^{-6}$ is 4.5 mm on a bed at 4500 m, too loose where a millimetre of head is a millimetre of water. And where a limit holds an iterate almost still, the head test passes although the row is not solved. Under "implicit_upwind" faces, ConsistentGapCoupling and UnfilledCavities (the defaults), with no $N$ clamp that can bind (Clamps on the effective pressure), the water must therefore balance as well. In every grounded cell the residual

\[r_P = \mathcal{W}_P(h^{(k+1)}) + b_{e,P}^{(k+1)} - W_P^{(m)} - b_{e,P}^{(m)} - \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} - (\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q})_P \right)\]

is the change of the total water thickness over the step minus the water that the sources and the face fluxes bring. It must satisfy $|r_P| < \epsilon_w\, \Sigma_P$ with $\epsilon_w = \max(\epsilon_h, 10^{-3})$ and

\[\Sigma_P = \mathcal{W}_P(h^{(k+1)}) + \left| b_{e,P}^{(k+1)} - b_{e,P}^{(m)} \right| + W_P^{(m)} + \Delta t \left( \left| \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} \right| + \left| i_{e\to b,P} \right| \right) ,\]

the size of the cell's own water and sources. The face fluxes are left out of $\Sigma_P$, since a cell the water flows through can carry many times its own input. A head error far below the head tolerance can then change its water thickness by a large fraction of that input. The residual $r_P$ is also the difference between two water thicknesses at the end of the step, the one the head gives through $\mathcal{W}_P$ and the one the budget of Water and the budget gives. Where the test passes, the head and the budget agree on the water of the cell.

Two exceptions keep the test from failing on round-off and on donor switching. A cell also passes when $|r_P|$ is within 100 machine epsilons of the sum of all its terms, fluxes included. And after ten iterates whose head has converged but whose water has not, the test is relaxed to $10^{-2}$ of all the cell's terms, fluxes included. This admits cells next to a face whose donor flips, whose residual levels off near $10^{-3}$ of the throughflow.

Both tests can instead be made absolute with atol > 0 (off by default), which replaces the relative ones. The head test then asks for $\max_P |h^{(k+1)}_P - h^{(k)}_P|$ below atol in metres of head, and the water test for $\max_P |r_P|$ below atol in metres of water.

Under "implicit_upwind", once the head test passes, a face whose donor was held on its downstream side is moved to its upstream side and the iteration resumes, at most three times per step (Donors near a flow divide). A step that does not converge within the maximum number of iterates is accepted and counted, or retried at $\Delta t/2$ under the adaptive time step (Time-step control). The first iterate of a step is the head extrapolated in time from the last converged steps (The first iterate).

Kinematic waves

A kinematic wave appears wherever the flux of a quantity $W$ depends only on how much of it is there, and not on how it changes in space, $\partial W/\partial x$. With a flux $F(W)$ the balance $\partial W/\partial t + \partial F(W)/\partial x = 0$ becomes, by the chain rule,

\[\frac{\partial W}{\partial t} + c\, \frac{\partial W}{\partial x} = 0, \qquad c = \frac{\mathrm dF}{\mathrm dW} ,\]

which carries $W$ along at the speed $c$. A bump of water then moves downstream at that speed, and the bump is the wave. It is called kinematic because its speed follows from the relation between the flux and the amount alone, with no force or momentum balance.

In the sheet the wave comes from water on a sloping bed. An unfilled layer flows downslope under the bed slope $s$ with the flux $K(W)\, s$, and thicker water carries more, so $c = \mathrm d(K s)/\mathrm dW$. In laminar flow $K$ grows like $W^3$ and $c = 3|q|/W$, three times the mean velocity of the water $|q|/W$, so a pulse of water travels downslope faster than the water itself. Since thicker water moves faster, the back of a pulse catches up with its front and the front steepens, while the diffusion from $\partial W/\partial x$ spreads it again.

To see what the wave means for the time step, take a row of cells in which each cell drains to its downstream neighbour, with a flux $c\, W$ out of every cell (LeVeque, 2002). Over a step the water balance of cell $i$, with $i-1$ its upstream neighbour, is

\[W_{i}^{(m+1)} = W_{i}^{(m)} - r\, \big(W_{i} - W_{i-1}\big), \qquad r = \frac{c\, \Delta t}{\Delta x} ,\]

where $r$ is the distance the wave travels in one step, measured in cells. A scheme that is explicit in the wave takes the outflow and the inflow at the start of the step, so

\[W_{i}^{(m+1)} = (1 - r)\, W_{i}^{(m)} + r\, W_{i-1}^{(m)} .\]

The cell keeps the fraction $1 - r$ of its water and receives the fraction $r$ of its neighbour's. For $r > 1$ the cell sends out more water than it holds, its water thickness turns negative, and the swings grow from step to step. The explicit scheme therefore needs $r \le 1$, that is $\Delta t \le \Delta x / c$, so the wave may cross at most one cell per step. A scheme that is implicit in the wave takes the outflow and the inflow at the end of the step, so

\[W_{i}^{(m+1)} = \frac{W_{i}^{(m)} + r\, W_{i-1}^{(m+1)}}{1 + r} .\]

The new water thickness is a weighted average of the cell's old value and its upstream neighbour's new value, with positive weights that add to 1. It cannot leave the range of these two values, so nothing grows at any time step and a positive water thickness stays positive. The cost is that each step mixes a cell with its upstream neighbour, which spreads a sharp front over several cells, more so at large $r$. Shakti.jl offers both (Why the water thickness is differentiated).

Why the water thickness is differentiated

Under "implicit_upwind" a face takes the water thickness of its donor at the end of the step, and the row differentiates that water thickness in the donor's head (Newton terms of the donors). This section shows why both are needed.

In an unfilled cell the water pressure is zero, so the hydraulic head is $\hat{h} = z_b + W$. In one dimension, with the bed slope $s = -\partial z_b / \partial x$, the flux is $q = K(W)\, s - K(W)\, \partial W / \partial x$, and the water balance becomes

\[\frac{\partial W}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(K(W)\, s\big) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(K(W)\, \frac{\partial W}{\partial x}\right) + \frac{\dot m}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b} .\]

The second term on the left is a kinematic wave (Kinematic waves), which carries the water downslope at the speed $c = \mathrm d(K s)/\mathrm dW$. The flux grows like $W^3$ in laminar and like $W^{3/2}$ in fully turbulent flow, so $c$ lies between $\tfrac32 |q|/W$ and $3|q|/W$. The right-hand side adds a nonlinear diffusion. In an uplifted cell the hydraulic head is the overburden head plus the water thickness, $\hat{h} = h_o + W$, and the same holds with the slope of $h_o$ in place of the bed slope.

With "upwind" the face takes the donor's water thickness at the start of the step, so the scheme is explicit in the wave, and its time step is limited to $\Delta t \lesssim \Delta x / c$ (Kinematic waves). With $c = 3|q|/W$ in laminar flow this is

\[\Delta t \lesssim \frac{\Delta x\, W}{3 |q|} .\]

For a layer of 5 mm carrying $10^{-4}$ m² s⁻¹ at $\Delta x = 100$ m this is about half an hour. The limit comes with the unfilled and uplifted regimes. On the filled sheet the water thickness is the gap, which changes only through the gap update, so the filled sheet does not have it. Shakti.jl warns when "upwind" is chosen with unfilled cavities or uplift.

"implicit_upwind" removes the limit. It builds the transmissivity of each face from its donor's water thickness at the end of the step, $W_d = \mathcal{W}_d(h_d)$, which depends on the unknown head of the donor. With the Newton terms $J_f$, the step is the implicit scheme of Kinematic waves, so the water thickness does not grow at any time step. The cost is that sharp fronts are spread over several cells.

Taking the water thickness at the end of the step without its derivative does not remove the limit. Consider one unfilled cell with an inflow $Q$ per unit area, draining through one face to a neighbour of fixed hydraulic head $\hat{h}_F$ that lies $\Delta z = z_b - \hat{h}_F \ge 0$ below its bed. If each linear solve takes the transmissivity at the water thickness of the previous iterate, without its derivative, the row and the gradient of the map from one iterate to the next are

\[\frac{W^{(k+1)} - W^{(m)}}{\Delta t} + \frac{K(W^{(k)})}{\Delta x^2}\left(\Delta z + W^{(k+1)}\right) = Q, \qquad \frac{\mathrm dW^{(k+1)}}{\mathrm dW^{(k)}} = -\frac{(\mathrm dK/\mathrm dW)(W^{(k)})\,(\Delta z + W^{(k+1)})/\Delta x^2}{1/\Delta t + K(W^{(k)})/\Delta x^2} .\]

The iteration converges only where this gradient is smaller than 1 in magnitude. At small $\Delta t$ it is about $-r$, which represents the (negative of the) distance the wave travels in one step in cells (Kinematic waves), so the iteration converges only below the explicit limit. At large $\Delta t$ it tends to $-(\mathrm dK/\mathrm dW)(\Delta z + W)/K$, which is $-3(W + \Delta z)/W$ in laminar flow. Its magnitude is at least 3, so each iterate moves further from the solution. A Newton step includes $\mathrm dK/\mathrm dW$, and its map has gradient zero at the solution, so near the solution it converges at any $\Delta t$.

Gap update

FullyImplicitGapScheme evaluates both the opening and the closure at $b^{(m+1)}$:

\[b^{(m+1)} = b^{(m)} + \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + \gamma \left(\mathcal{B} - b^{(m+1)}\right)_+ - C\, b^{(m+1)} \right), \qquad \gamma = \frac{|\mathbf{u}_b|}{l_r}, \quad C = A |N|^{n-1} N ,\]

at $N = N^{(m+1)}$ and $\dot m = \dot m^{(m+1)}$, with $\mathcal{B}$ the bump height of Gap height. The equation is piecewise linear in $b^{(m+1)}$, so it is solved in closed form:

\[b^{(m+1)} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{b^{(m)} + \Delta t\, (\dot m / \rho_i + \gamma \mathcal{B})}{1 + \Delta t\, (\gamma + C)} & \text{if this is } < \mathcal{B} , \\[2ex] \max\!\left(\dfrac{b^{(m)} + \Delta t\, \dot m / \rho_i}{1 + \Delta t\, C},\; \mathcal{B}\right) & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

The result is clamped to $[b_{\min}, b_{\max}]$, and the update holds for any $\Delta t$.

ExplicitGapScheme is forward Euler, $b^{(m+1)} = b^{(m)} + \Delta t\, \dot b(N^{(m+1)}, b^{(m)})$, accurate and free of overshoot only for $\Delta t\, (\gamma + C) < 1$.

On the filled sheet without uplift the water thickness is the gap, $W = b$, and the gap cannot close below $b_{\min}$. A cell that its fluxes drain would therefore keep $b_{\min}$ of water whatever its head. Its water-thickness function would have zero gradient with respect to $h$ at the clamp, so its row would lose the term $(\mathrm d\mathcal{W}/\mathrm dh)/\Delta t$ from its diagonal, and the cell would keep its transmissivity $K(b_{\min})$ and pass on its neighbours' water at an almost flat head. The results would then depend on the choice of $b_{\min}$. Under ConsistentGapCoupling without gap diffusion, Shakti.jl therefore lets the water drain below the gap. The new water thickness $W^{(m+1)}$ is the gap update of the chosen scheme, the closed form of FullyImplicitGapScheme or the forward Euler step of ExplicitGapScheme, with $W^{(m)}$ in place of $b^{(m)}$ and clamped to $[0, b_{\max}]$, and the gap is $b^{(m+1)} = \max(W^{(m+1)}, b_{\min})$. Where $W^{(m+1)} \ge b_{\min}$ this is the ordinary filled sheet, $W = b$. Where $W^{(m+1)} < b_{\min}$ the gap holds $b_{\min} - W^{(m+1)}$ of empty space, the dry floor. The filled regime of the head row and the transmissivity of each face take the same $W^{(m+1)}$, so the row and the gap update agree on the water, and a drained cell carries no more water. The results then no longer depend on $b_{\min}$.

Accuracy of the gap update

Each case of the closed form, below the bump and above it, is a backward-Euler step of a linear equation, that is, a step that takes the rate at the end of the step, at $b^{(m+1)}$. The equation is

\[\dot b = S - R\, b ,\]

with $S = \dot m/\rho_i + \gamma \mathcal{B}$ and $R = \gamma + C$ below the bump, and $S = \dot m/\rho_i$ and $R = C$ above it. Both are held fixed over the step. With them fixed, the gap would settle at $b^* = S/R$, the gap at which opening and closure balance. Write $e = b - b^*$ for the distance of the gap from that value. Since $S = R\, b^*$, the equation becomes $\dot e = -R\, e$, so the distance decays exponentially at the rate $R$, and $1/R$ is the relaxation time of the cell.

Over one step, both the exact solution and the update multiply the distance by a factor. The exact solution multiplies it by $\exp(-R\Delta t)$, and the update by $1/(1 + R\Delta t)$.

\[e^{(m+1)} = \exp(-R\Delta t)\, e^{(m)} \quad \text{(exact)}, \qquad e^{(m+1)} = \frac{e^{(m)}}{1 + R\Delta t} \quad \text{(update)} .\]

The second follows from the backward-Euler step $(e^{(m+1)} - e^{(m)})/\Delta t = -R\, e^{(m+1)}$, or by subtracting $b^*$ from both sides of the closed form above. Forward Euler (ExplicitGapScheme) gives $e^{(m+1)} = (1 - R\Delta t)\, e^{(m)}$ instead, which changes sign for $R\Delta t > 1$ (the gap overshoots $b^*$) and grows for $R\Delta t > 2$ (the update diverges).

For small $R\Delta t$ the two factors agree in their first two terms, $1 - R\Delta t$, and differ by about $\tfrac12 (R\Delta t)^2$. Each step therefore incurs an error of about $\tfrac12 (R\Delta t)^2 e^{(m)}$, and over a run of length $T$ the $T/\Delta t$ steps add up to an error proportional to $\Delta t$ and to $R^2$. The update is first-order accurate in time, like forward Euler.

For large $R\Delta t$ both factors are close to zero, so the exact solution and the update both put the gap close to $b^*$. The error lies in how fast the gap approaches $b^*$, not in the value it approaches. Where the ice is thick and the water pressure is far below overburden, $N$ is of order 10 MPa and $1/R$ is of order minutes. There the true gap reaches $b^*$ within minutes, so at a time step of hours the update and the true gap agree.

The analysis holds for $R \ge 0$. For $R < 0$ the distance from $b^*$ grows instead of decaying, and Shakti.jl replaces the update by the exact solution over the step (Negative effective pressure).

Derivative of the gap update

In a filled cell the water thickness at the end of the step is the gap the update leaves, or, on the dry floor, the water the update leaves, $\mathcal{W}_P(h) = \mathcal{G}_P(N)$ with $N = p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)$. The head equation linearizes this water thickness about the iterate (Picard iteration), so it needs the gradient

\[\frac{\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P}{\mathrm d h} = -\rho_w g\, \frac{\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P}{\mathrm d N} .\]

Where the gap is not at $b_{\mathrm{min}}$ or $b_{\mathrm{max}}$, differentiating the backward-Euler update $b^{(m+1)} = (b^{(m)} + \Delta t\, S)/(1 + \Delta t\, R)$ through the creep rate $C = A |N|^{n-1} N$, which enters $R$, gives

\[\frac{\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P}{\mathrm d N} = -\frac{n A |N|^{n-1}\, \Delta t\, \mathcal{G}_P}{1 + \Delta t\, R} \le 0 .\]

A larger effective pressure closes the gap faster, so the update leaves a smaller gap. Where the gap is at $b_{\mathrm{min}}$ or $b_{\mathrm{max}}$ it does not change with $N$, and the gradient is zero.

The row puts this gradient divided by $\Delta t$ on its diagonal, and two limits show what it means. For $\Delta t\, R \ll 1$, $\Delta t^{-1}\, \mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P / \mathrm d h$ tends to $\rho_w g\, n A |N|^{n-1} b^{(m)}$, the Newton term of the creep closure in the row of ExplicitGapCoupling (Gap couplings). For $\Delta t\, R \gg 1$ and no sliding, $\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P / \mathrm d h \approx \rho_w g\, n\, \mathcal{G}_P / N$, so the diagonal term vanishes as $\Delta t$ grows. Where the ice is thick and $N$ is large, the gap reaches its balance value within a step, so $\mathcal{G}_P$ and its gradient are small, and the diagonal of a filled cell is small. This is one reason the iteration limits its steps (Limiting the head iterate).

Shakti.jl does not code this formula. It evaluates the gradient by a central difference (scheme_head_terms),

\[\frac{\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P}{\mathrm d N} \approx \frac{\mathcal{G}_P(N + \delta) - \mathcal{G}_P(N - \delta)}{2\delta}, \qquad \delta = 10^{-6} \max\!\left(|N|,\, 10^{3}\ \mathrm{Pa}\right),\]

and sets a positive value of it to zero, so that $\mathrm d \mathcal{G}_P / \mathrm d h \ge 0$. One formula then covers both cases of the update, the exponential step at negative effective pressure (Negative effective pressure), the clamps, the creep cutoff and the dry floor of the filled sheet, each of which would otherwise need its own derivative. The floor of $10^3$ Pa in $\delta$ keeps the step from vanishing at $N = 0$.

Negative effective pressure

Each case of the update is a step of the linear equation $\dot b = S - R\, b$ (Accuracy of the gap update). Where $N < 0$ the water pressure exceeds the weight of the ice, the creep rate $C$ is negative, and creep opens the gap instead of closing it. The rate $R$ can then be negative. Above the bump $R = C < 0$ whenever $N < 0$, and below it $R = \gamma + C < 0$ where sliding is slow. With $R < 0$ the distance of the gap from $b^*$ grows over the step instead of decaying. Backward Euler would divide by $1 - \Delta t\, |R|$. That denominator vanishes at $\Delta t = 1/|R|$, where the update blows up, and is negative beyond it, where the update gives a negative gap.

For $R < 0$ Shakti.jl therefore integrates that case exactly over the step, with $S$ and $R$ held fixed. Writing $R = -|R|$, the equation is $\dot b = S + |R|\, b$. Multiplying it by $\exp(-|R|\, t)$ gives

\[\frac{d}{dt}\Big(b\, \exp(-|R|\, t)\Big) = S\, \exp(-|R|\, t) .\]

Integrating over the step, from $b(t^{(m)}) = b^{(m)}$ to $b(t^{(m+1)}) = b^{(m+1)}$, and multiplying by $\exp(|R|\, t^{(m+1)})$ leaves only the step length $\Delta t = t^{(m+1)} - t^{(m)}$,

\[b^{(m+1)} = b^{(m)} \exp(x) + S\, \Delta t\, \frac{\exp(x) - 1}{x}, \qquad x = |R|\, \Delta t .\]

The code evaluates $\exp(x) - 1$ with the library function expm1. For small $x$, $\exp(x) - 1$ subtracts two nearly equal numbers and loses digits, which expm1 avoids. As $R \to 0^-$, $(\exp(x) - 1)/x \to 1$ and the update tends to $b^{(m)} + S\, \Delta t$, the backward-Euler value at $R = 0$. The update is therefore continuous in $R$, and it stays finite for any time step that does not overflow the exponential ($x \gtrsim 700$). This is one reason the iteration limits how far $N$ may fall below zero in one iterate (Limiting the head iterate). The result is clamped to $[b_{\min}, b_{\max}]$ as in the other cases.

The update for $R \ge 0$ keeps backward Euler. The exponential step is exact only while $S$, $R$ and the case stay fixed over the step. Backward Euler instead evaluates them at the gap at the end of the step, so it picks the case the new gap lies in, and with the creep cutoff (Creep cutoff) it also covers the parts of the range where the closure is quadratic in $b$. Both schemes are first-order accurate in time, since $N$ changes over the step anyway.

The floor $\max(\cdot, \mathcal{B})$ in the second case of the update matters only at $N < 0$. For $C \ge 0$, a first case at or above $\mathcal{B}$ implies a second case at or above $\mathcal{B}$ too, so the floor never binds. For $C < 0$ this no longer holds, and the exponential step of the second case can land below $\mathcal{B}$ after the first case has been rejected. The update then returns $\mathcal{B}$, which keeps it continuous in $b^{(m)}$ and $N$.

Creep cutoff

With $b_c > 0$ the creep length is $l_c = b^2/b_c$ for $b \le b_c$ and $b$ above (Gap height), so the closure $C\, l_c$ is quadratic in $b$ below $b_c$, and very thin gaps close more slowly, since $l_c = b^2/b_c$ is smaller than $b$ there. The rate of the gap changes its formula at two gap values, the bump height $\mathcal{B}$, above which sliding no longer opens the gap, and $b_c$, above which $l_c = b$. These two thresholds divide the range of $b$ into four parts, and backward Euler gives one candidate gap on each. Above $b_c$ the candidates are the solutions of the two cases of Gap update, below and above the bump. Below $b_c$ they are the positive roots of

\[\frac{\Delta t\, C}{b_c}\, b^2 + (1 + \Delta t\, \gamma_j)\, b - \left(b^{(m)} + \Delta t\, S_j\right) = 0, \qquad j = 1, 2,\]

with $\gamma_1 = \gamma$ and $S_1 = \dot m/\rho_i + \gamma \mathcal{B}$ below the bump, and $\gamma_2 = 0$ and $S_2 = \dot m/\rho_i$ above it. For $C > 0$ the residual of the update, $b - b^{(m)}$ minus $\Delta t$ times the rate at $b$, increases with $b$, so exactly one candidate lies in the part of the range it was derived for. The code tests the candidates in turn. The solution can lie exactly on a threshold. The candidates of the two parts that meet there then come out within rounding of the threshold, and can each fall just on the wrong side of it, so that no candidate lies in its own part. The code then returns the threshold, $b_c$ or $\mathcal{B}$, at which the residual is smaller.

Writing the quadratic as $a_2 b^2 + a_1 b - a_0 = 0$ with $a_2, a_0 > 0$, its positive root is computed as

\[b = \frac{2 a_0}{a_1 + \sqrt{a_1^2 + 4 a_2 a_0}} ,\]

the same form as the root for the Reynolds number in Face flux, which avoids subtracting two nearly equal numbers when $4 a_2 a_0 \ll a_1^2$, at small time steps and thin gaps.

For $C < 0$, where creep opens the gap, the gap updates use $l_c = b$. An opening rate $|C|\, b^2/b_c$ grows faster than the gap itself and would make the gap infinite in a finite time. The explicit-coupling rows and the gap-diffusion system use $l_c$ from the start of the step, and choose its form from the sign of $N$ at the end of the previous step.

Gap diffusion

Under WithDiffusion the gap equation gains the wall-melt term $\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{D}\nabla b)$ (Gap height), which couples neighbouring cells. No cell can then compute its gap alone, and the gap update becomes a second linear system, that of SUHMO (Felden et al., 2023),

\[\left(1 + \sum_f d_f + \delta_P\right) b^{(m+1)}_P - \sum_{f:\,F\ \text{grounded}} d_f\, b^{(m+1)}_F = b^{(m)}_P + \Delta t\, s_{b,P}, \qquad d_f = \frac{\Delta t\, \mathcal{D}_f}{\Delta_f^2},\]

with

\[\delta_P = \Delta t\, (C)_+ \frac{l_c}{b^{(m)}} + \Delta t\, \gamma_\beta, \qquad s_{b,P} = \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + (-C)_+\, l_c + \gamma_\beta\, \mathcal{B},\]

where $(x)_+ = \max(x, 0)$, and $\gamma_\beta = \gamma$ where the lagged $\beta$ is positive and 0 elsewhere. Closure is implicit for $C > 0$, through the lagged ratio $l_c / b^{(m)}$ (exact for $l_c = b$), and explicit for $C \le 0$. Opening by sliding is implicit where the lagged $\beta$ is positive, and the melt is explicit. SUHMO instead takes the closure and the opening by sliding at the start-of-step gap, on the right-hand side, and only the diffusion at the new gap. Both terms are linear in the new gap and add only positive entries to the diagonal, as in FullyImplicitGapScheme, so taking them at the new gap keeps the properties of the matrix below. Faces to cells that are not grounded carry no diffusion. The matrix is symmetric, with a diagonal of at least $1 + \sum_f d_f$, so its eigenvalues are at least 1. It is positive definite and well conditioned, and it takes its own solver (Linear solvers). The result is clamped to $[b_{\min}, b_{\max}]$.

The diffusion coefficient $\mathcal{D}$ is built per cell from the dissipation and the sensible heat of Melt on the grid,

\[\mathcal{D}_P = \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{\min(\max(W_P, 0),\, b_P)}{\rho_i L} \left( Q_{\mathrm{diss},P} + Q_{\mathrm{sens},P} \right)\right), \qquad \mathcal{D}_f = \max\!\left(\tfrac12 (\mathcal{D}_P + \mathcal{D}_F),\; \mathcal{D}_{\min}\right) .\]

$\min(W, b)$ is the height of the gap walls that the water wets, the whole gap in a filled cell and only the water thickness in an unfilled one. In an uplifted cell it is the gap $b$. The water above the gap is the uplift layer, a sheet under the lifted ice that spans the whole cell, so it adds no side wall to melt. The floor at zero matters on an adverse bed slope, where the sensible heat is negative. A negative $\mathcal{D}$ would be backward diffusion, which has no stable solution. The cell values are averaged to the faces, which take at least D_min (m² s⁻¹, default 0), set as WithDiffusion(ls; D_min, D_faces). Averaging cell values lets a channel that runs along one grid direction also diffuse sideways, across it. A coefficient built per face from that face's own flux would take the small flux on the faces beside the channel instead of the channel's heat, so the channel would hardly widen. A diagonal channel crosses both kinds of face and would widen, so the result would depend on the orientation of the grid. With D_faces = true the face coefficient is instead the mean wet gap of the two cells times the mean of their heats, as in SUHMO,

\[\mathcal{D}_f = \max\!\left(\frac{w_P + w_F}{2}\, \frac{H_P^+ + H_F^+}{2}\, \frac{1}{\rho_i L},\; \mathcal{D}_{\min}\right), \qquad w = \min(\max(W, 0),\, b), \quad H = Q_{\mathrm{diss}} + Q_{\mathrm{sens}}, \quad H^+ = \max(H, 0) .\]

The two forms differ only in the order of averaging and multiplying. At the face between a channel cell $P$ and a dry neighbour $F$, with $w_F = 0$ and $H_F = 0$, the default gives $\mathcal{D}_f = \mathcal{D}_P/2$ and D_faces = true gives $\mathcal{D}_f = \mathcal{D}_P/4$, so SUHMO's form spreads a channel into dry ground half as fast.

Under ConsistentGapCoupling each head row stores the water thickness its cell will hold at the end of the step, which in a filled cell is the new gap (Gap couplings). Without diffusion the new gap of a cell depends only on its own effective pressure, $\mathcal{G}_P(N)$, so the row can compute it from its own head. With diffusion it also depends on the new gaps of the neighbours, through the term $d_f\, b_F^{(m+1)}$ of the gap system, and theirs depend on their own neighbours. The new gap of one cell then depends on the heads of all cells, and only a solve of the whole gap system gives it. Shakti.jl therefore solves the gap system inside the Picard iteration, in the following steps.

  1. After each head solve, the gap system is solved at the new $N$, $\dot m$ and $\mathcal{D}$. This gives the new gap of every cell, $b_{\mathrm{next}}$.
  2. The next head row of each cell stores $b_{\mathrm{next}}$ as the water thickness at the end of the step, so the water the cell gains over the step is $b_{\mathrm{next}} - W^{(m)}$. In an unfilled or uplifted cell it adds $h - z_b$ or $h - h_o$ as in Gap couplings.
  3. The row also needs the gradient of $b_{\mathrm{next}}$ with respect to the cell's head, its Newton term. The exact gradient would include how the neighbours' new gaps change with this cell's head, which would need a further solve. Shakti.jl instead takes the cell's own row of the gap system, $b_P = (b^{(m)}_P + \Delta t\, s_{b,P} + \sum_f d_f\, b_F)/(1 + \sum_f d_f + \delta_P)$, holds the neighbours' gaps $b_F$ at $b_{\mathrm{next}}$, and differentiates it in $N$ by the central difference of Derivative of the gap update. The Newton term then keeps the cell's dependence on its own head and drops the dependence through its neighbours. Like the other held terms of the Picard iteration, this changes how fast the iteration converges, not the head it converges to, since the converged row stores the $b_{\mathrm{next}}$ of the whole system.
  4. Under "implicit_upwind" faces (the default), the transmissivity of each face is built from its donor's water thickness at the end of the step, so it needs the new gap too. The fluxes are computed from the transmissivity, and $\dot m$ and $\mathcal{D}$ from the fluxes, so this gap cannot wait for them. After each head solve the gap system is therefore solved twice. The first solve uses the new $N$ with the $\dot m$ and $\mathcal{D}$ of the previous iterate, and gives the gap for the transmissivity. The fluxes, $\dot m$ and $\mathcal{D}$ are then updated, and the second solve gives the gap for the next head rows (step 1). The transmissivity takes the smaller of the start-of-step gap $b^{(m)}$ and the gap of the first solve. Where the gap closes, it takes the new gap, so a closing cell does not pass on water it no longer holds. Where the gap opens, it keeps $b^{(m)}$, so the opening reaches the transmissivity only at the next step. The melt in the transmissivity lags in the same way (Melt in the transmissivity), which keeps a growing gap from raising the flux, the melt and the gap again within one step.

The same solves are made once before the first iterate, at the extrapolated head. A step of $K$ iterates therefore solves the gap system $K + 1$ times, and $2K + 2$ times under "implicit_upwind". The last solve, at the converged head, is the gap update of the step, $b^{(m+1)} = b_{\mathrm{next}}$, so the head rows and the gap update agree on the water.

Under ExplicitGapCoupling the head rows take the gap change from the rate of Gap couplings, evaluated at the start-of-step gap, and add $-\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{D}\nabla b^{(m)})$, the diffusion of the start-of-step gap, to the right-hand side. The gap system then moves the gap with the closure, the opening by sliding and the diffusion of the end-of-step gap. The gap the rows assume and the gap the system returns therefore differ, and the water between them is created or lost. The difference grows with $\Delta t$, so this coupling does not conserve water at long time steps. This is the coupling of SUHMO, which solves the head with the gap held at its start-of-step value and then solves the gap system once at the new head (Felden et al., 2023). It costs one gap solve per step, against $K + 1$ or $2K + 2$ under ConsistentGapCoupling, and suits short time steps or reproducing SUHMO.

Water and the budget

Under UnfilledCavities the water thickness is advanced with the converged fluxes,

\[W^{(m+1)}_P = \max\!\left(0,\; W^{(m)}_P + \Delta t \left( \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} - (\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q})_P \right) - \left( b_e(h^{(m+1)}_P) - b^{(m)}_{e,P} \right) \right),\]

and $b^{(m+1)}_e = b_e(h^{(m+1)})$ is stored for the next step. The gap is updated separately (Gap update), so the empty part of the gap is $b^{(m+1)} - W^{(m+1)}$, and any water above the gap is the uplift layer. On the other sheets the water thickness is not advanced from the budget. On the dry floor (FilledCavities, NoUplift and ConsistentGapCoupling without gap diffusion) the gap update sets $W^{(m+1)}$ (Gap update). Otherwise the water thickness is the new gap, plus the uplift layer under IceUplift, $W^{(m+1)} = b^{(m+1)} + (h^{(m+1)} - h_o)_+$. Under NoUplift it is the gap alone, $W^{(m+1)} = b^{(m+1)}$, even where $h^{(m+1)} > h_o$. Under ConsistentGapCoupling this is $\mathcal{W}_P(h^{(m+1)})$.

Conservation

The two cells of a face read the same flux $q_f$. Both rows use the same $K_f$ and the same difference of $\hat{h}$, so the water that leaves one cell through the face enters the other. Summed over any set of cells, the fluxes of its interior faces cancel, and the water the set holds changes only by its sources and by the flux through its outer faces. This holds at every head, not only at convergence, so the fluxes never create or lose water. For the same reason $K_f$ is computed once per face and iterate and the same value is used in the matrix, the melt rate and the budget below.

Summed over all grounded cells, the water under grounded ice, the total water thickness times the cell area, $V^{(m)} = \sum_P \left(W^{(m)}_P + b^{(m)}_{e,P}\right) \Delta x \Delta y$, changes over a step as

\[V^{(m+1)} - V^{(m)} = \Delta t \left( I - O \right) + V_{\mathrm{created}} , \qquad I = \sum_P \left( \frac{\dot m_P}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b,P} \right) \Delta x \Delta y , \qquad O = \sum_{f \to \text{land, ocean}} q_f \, \Delta s_f ,\]

where $O$ sums the outflow through the faces to LAND and OCEAN cells and $\Delta s_f$ is the length of the face ($\Delta y$ for an $x$ face). Faces to OTHER_BASIN, FROZEN_BED and the edge of the grid carry no flux. $V_{\mathrm{created}}$ is the water a step leaves beyond its sources and fluxes. It has three sources.

  1. Under UnfilledCavities, the floor $W \ge 0$ of the update above. A cell whose fluxes take out more water than it holds has its water thickness set to zero, which creates the missing water. With "upwind" faces this happens above the time-step limit of the rule, and with "implicit_upwind" faces only in unconverged steps (Why the water thickness is differentiated). The averaging rules ("arithmetic", "harmonic", "gap_mean") would do so at any time step, which is why they are refused under UnfilledCavities. Where the head rows and the gap update disagree on the water, as under ExplicitGapCoupling or in an unconverged step, the difference stays in $W - b$, as empty gap or as water above the gap, and creates water only where it drives $W$ below zero.
  2. On the other sheets, the coupling. Their water thickness is taken from the head and the gap, not advanced with the fluxes, so it differs from the budget wherever the head rows and the gap update disagree on the water. Under ExplicitGapCoupling they disagree wherever creep is fast or a clamp at $b_{\min}$ or $b_{\max}$ binds (Gap couplings). Under ConsistentGapCoupling they agree once the step converges.
  3. On the other sheets, an unconverged step. Its water thickness keeps the residual of its rows.

water_created(sim) returns $V_{\mathrm{created}}$ of the last step and its sums over all steps, also summed by sign (gross_created, gross_lost) so that water created in one cell or step and lost in another does not cancel. Under ConsistentGapCoupling a converged step creates water of the order of the Picard tolerance times the input, and under ExplicitGapCoupling percents of the input or more.

Where the water test applies (Convergence tests), it also bounds what a converged step can create. Each cell is balanced to $\epsilon_w \Sigma_P$, so

\[\left| V_{\mathrm{created}} \right| \lesssim \epsilon_w \sum_P \Sigma_P \, \Delta x \Delta y ,\]

with $\Sigma_P$ the cell's water in the gap at the start and the end of the step, the change of its englacial water, and the magnitudes of its melt (or freeze-on) and input from above over the step. The englacial water itself and the face fluxes do not enter. This is a worst case, far above the water converged steps create in practice. But it is set by the water in the gap as well as by the sources, so where the gap holds much more water than the sources bring in one step, the bound allows a created water that is a sizeable fraction of that step's input. Water removed by mask changes is not part of it (Mask changes).

Melt on the grid

The melt rate of a cell is

\[\dot m_P = \max\!\left( \frac{Q_{G,P} + Q_{b,P} + Q_{\mathrm{diss},P} + Q_{\mathrm{sens},P} - Q_{T,P}}{L},\; \dot m_{\min,P} \right),\]

with the heat terms of Melt rate and, with limit_freeze_on (the default), the floor $\dot m_{\min} = -\rho_i C_{\mathrm{cap}}$ of Freeze-on capacity. Each heat term can be switched off, and it is then not computed at all. With gap_opening_melt = :exclude_uniform the gap opens by less than $\dot m/\rho_i$, since part of the uniform melt makes water but no gap, and the floor then applies to the part that opens the gap (Melt in the gap opening).

The floor keeps a freezing cell from freezing its gap shut below $b_{\min}$, or from freezing more water than it holds, within one step. It is computed once per step from the start-of-step state, again at $\Delta t/2$ when the adaptive time step retries a step, and held during the iteration, so the head rows see a fixed limit.

The heat terms are built from face quantities, the flux and the gradients, which the rest of this section turns into cell values.

The dissipation and the sensible heat are built from face products. On the staggered grid both factors of $\mathbf{q}\cdot\nabla\hat{h}$ and of $\mathbf{q}\cdot\nabla p_w$ live on the faces. On face $f$, $q_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f$ is the power dissipated per unit bed area in a strip of width $\Delta_f$ centred on the face. That strip lies half in each of the two cells, so each cell receives half of it,

\[Q_{\mathrm{diss},P} = \rho_w g \left| \sum_f s_f\, q_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f \right|, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{sens},P} = c_t c_w \rho_w \sum_f s_f\, q_f (\nabla p_w)_f ,\]

with $s_f = \tfrac12$ on a face between two grounded cells. A face to an OCEAN or LAND cell has $s_f = 1$. The boundary cell has no hydrology to receive its half of the heat of the outflow, so the grounded cell takes all of it. For uniform $\mathbf{q}$ and $\nabla\hat{h}$ the two $x$ faces of a cell contribute $q_x \partial_x\hat{h}$ and the two $y$ faces $q_y \partial_y\hat{h}$, so the sum gives $\mathbf{q}\cdot\nabla\hat{h}$ exactly. Summed over all cells, the power of every face is counted once, so no heat is lost or created. Since $q_f = -K_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f$, every face product $q_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f = -K_f (\nabla\hat{h})_f^2$ is negative or zero, and the absolute value only turns the sum into a positive heat.

The frictional heat $Q_b = |\mathbf{u}_b\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_b|$ averages the face values of the sliding velocity times the shear stress, with weight $\tfrac12$ on every face, faces to land and ocean included,

\[Q_{b,P} = \left| \tfrac12 (u_x\tau_x)_{i+\frac12} + \tfrac12 (u_x\tau_x)_{i-\frac12} + \tfrac12 (u_y\tau_y)_{j+\frac12} + \tfrac12 (u_y\tau_y)_{j-\frac12} \right| .\]

Friction, unlike dissipation, is made on the bed of the cell itself, not along the path between two cell centres. Beyond a face to land or ocean there is no ice-covered bed whose heat the grounded cell would have to take over. With uniform sliding the average gives $\mathbf{u}_b\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$ exactly. Giving the grounded cell the full value of a face to land or ocean, as for the dissipation, would count that face twice, and give 1.5 times the true heat for sliding normal to it. The shear stress on a face is computed from the face value of $N$, the mean of its two cells, or the grounded cell's own value at a boundary face, floored at zero.
