Performance

Wall times of the example runs on the AWI cluster albedo, from docs/make_figures.jl.

  • GPU: one NVIDIA A100, CUDSSDirectSolver(grid; structure = :general).
  • CPU: 8 threads on a 128-core node, LUDirectSolver(grid).
  • Both with PicardSolver(500, 1e-6, ...) and four output fields written at the first and last step.
  • Total is the wall time of run! for the whole simulation, all steps; per step is total divided by the number of steps. Compilation is excluded: each run does a one-step warm-up first.

Options:

  • defaults: UnfilledCavities(), IceUplift(), SheetTopPotential(), "implicit_upwind" faces, ConsistentGapCoupling(), b_min = 1e-6, b_max = Inf.
  • SHAKTI: the original equations, FilledCavities(), NoUplift(), BedPotential(), ExplicitGapCoupling(), b_min = 1e-3 (Drang Drung also b_max = 1), and "arithmetic" faces (the default without unfilled cavities or uplift).
  • Helheim and Drang Drung use the physical constants of their ISSM runs (see their pages); the ice sheets use the default constants.

¹ Timings only. On the ice sheets the original equations do not give a usable state after a year: where the water cannot fill the gap p_w falls far below zero (N/p_o up to 400 on Thwaites and 37 000 on Greenland 16 km), and on Greenland and Antarctica the gap runs away to 6 to 109 m. The defaults keep 0 ≤ p_w ≤ p_o and gaps below 1.1 m.

  • The cost is set by the number of Picard iterations a step, times one sparse direct solve of the head system. On the ice sheets the defaults need two to seven iterations a step at the end of the year.
  • Helheim with the defaults is the expensive case: more than ten iterations a step against one for the original equations.
  • The GPU is 6 to 23 times faster than the CPU here, more on the larger grids.
