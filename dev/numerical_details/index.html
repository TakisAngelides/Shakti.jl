Further numerical details

Discretization gives the discrete equations that a step solves. This page covers what surrounds them: the safeguards that let the Picard iteration converge at long time steps, the handling of cells that exchange no water, the time-step control, changes of the mask, and the linear solvers. None of it changes the solution of a converged step; it decides whether, and how fast, the step converges.

Upwind donors

Under "implicit_upwind" each face takes the conductance of its donor, the cell with the higher $\hat{h}$. Near a flow divide the head difference across a face is small, and the donor can switch at every iterate, which changes the matrix every time and can keep the iteration from settling. So the donors are tracked through the step:

  1. At the start of a step every face is free. A face takes the higher-$\hat{h}$ cell as its donor at its first iterate with flow, and switches each time the flow reverses.
  2. After four reversals within the step the face keeps its donor for the rest of the step.
  3. Once the head has converged, a face that was frozen on its downstream side (the flow now runs into the donor) is moved to its upstream side, and the iteration resumes. A face that reverses again after that takes the conductance of its thinner cell. This polishing runs at most three times per step.

The water-depth Newton terms (Picard iteration) follow the donor in use, so the matrix stays consistent with the conductance it was built from.

The donor's water depth is its water at the end of the step at the current head iterate. Its melt opening is the one at the start of the step, unless the melt at the iterate gives less water; this keeps the feedback $W \to K \to q \to \dot m \to W$ of a growing channel out of the iteration, while the head rows themselves use the melt at the iterate. On the filled sheet's dry floor the depth is the water the gap update leaves, not the gap held at $b_\min$, so a drained cell does not conduct. Under WithDiffusion it is the smaller of the start-of-step gap and the gap of the last gap solve.

Limiting the head iterate

Each head row has kinks where its slope jumps: the bed (unfilled to filled), the overburden head (filled to uplifted) and the boundary head of an outflow-only face. A Newton step taken from one side of a kink can land far into the other, and the next step lands far back, so the iteration cycles. After each linear solve the new head is limited cell by cell, in this order:

  • Filled cap. Under ConsistentGapCoupling the step of a filled grounded cell is capped at $|\Delta N| \le \max(|N|/2,\, p_o/4)$, and at least 1 m of head. On thick ice the gap update closes a thin gap within one step for most $N$, so near $N = p_o$ it is nearly flat in $N$ and the Newton step overshoots by kilometres. On the filled sheet without uplift, where $N$ may go negative, $N$ may fall at most $p_o/4$ below $\min(N^k, 0)$ per iterate, so it cannot run away geometrically. The cap is off on the filled sheet with englacial storage ($e_v > 0$), whose rows stay steep in $N$.
  • Regime stops. An update that crosses the bed or the overburden head stops just inside the new regime (by a millionth of the gap), so the next solve uses the row of the regime the head is in.
  • Limits inside a regime. An uplift layer at most doubles per iterate, and the head of an unfilled cell sinks below the bed at most twice as deep, or by three quarters of the gap if that is more.
  • Outflow stops. An update that crosses the boundary head of an outflow-only face stops at it, so the face is in the state it ends in for the next solve.
  • Oscillation damping. An update that reverses the previous one by more than half of it is cut to half, which brackets a root at a kink and halves the bracket each iterate, as bisection does.

A converged head is a fixed point of the limited iteration, so the limits do not change the solution. With UnderHeadRelaxation(alpha) the limited head is further blended with the previous one, $h^{k+1} \leftarrow \alpha h^{k+1} + (1-\alpha) h^k$; AndersonHeadRelaxation accelerates it instead.

The first iterate

The iteration starts from the head extrapolated in time through the last converged heads (head_extrapolation_order, 1 = linear by default), with Lagrange weights on the actual step end times so that a varying $\Delta t$ is handled exactly. Only converged heads enter the history: the first extrapolation happens at the third step, and a step that does not converge clears the history. Only grounded cells are extrapolated. The extrapolated head is then limited as above, measured from the last converged head and without the filled cap. A cell that changed regime in the last step moved by tens of metres, and extrapolating that move into the uplifted regime would start the step with a blister tens of metres deep.

Closed cells

A grounded cell whose four faces are all closed (frozen or OTHER_BASIN neighbours, closed outflow faces, the domain edge) exchanges no water. With $e_v = 0$ its row has no off-diagonal entries, and its diagonal, the slope of its stored water, is zero at $N = 0$ and tiny near it, where a Newton step overshoots by orders of magnitude. Its head is therefore solved exactly. The water at the end of the step is known, $T = W^n + \Delta t\,(\dot m/\rho_w + i_{e\to b})$, less the wall diffusion under WithDiffusion.

  • Under ConsistentGapCoupling, if uplift is allowed and $T > \mathcal{G}(0)$, the ice is lifted: $h = h_o + T - \mathcal{G}(0)$. If unfilled cavities are allowed and $T < \mathcal{G}(p_o)$, the gap is partly empty: $h = z_b - (\mathcal{G}(p_o) - T)$. Otherwise $\mathcal{G}(N) = T$ is solved for $N$ by 60 bisections, on $[0, p_o]$ on the GlaDS-2 sheet, and on $[-10 p_o, 10 p_o]$ on the filled sheet ($1000\, p_o$ on its dry floor), within $[N_\min, N_\max]$.
  • Under ExplicitGapCoupling the creep must close the gap as fast as sliding and melt open it, $A |N|^{n-1} N\, l_c = R$ with $R = \beta |\mathbf{u}_b| - \dot m (1/\rho_w - 1/\rho_i) - i_{e\to b}$ (plus $(b^n - W^n)/\Delta t$ on the GlaDS-2 sheet), so $N = \operatorname{sign}(R)\,(|R| / (A l_c))^{1/n}$, clamped to $[N_\min, N_\max]$. Without creep ($A l_c = 0$) the gap cannot close, and $N = p_o$ if $R > 0$, else 0. The unfilled and uplifted cases are taken first, as above: the gap is partly empty if even the closure at $p_o$ cannot balance $R$, and the ice is lifted if $R < 0$.

With $e_v > 0$ the englacial storage keeps every diagonal positive, and closed cells take the ordinary row. A patch of several cells closed as a whole, all at $N = 0$, has no head level of its own and is not covered.

Time-step control

Under FixedTimeStep every step has the same $\Delta t$. A step whose head solve does not converge is taken as it is, counted in water_created(sim).unconverged, and reported once with the water it created.

AdaptiveTimeStep(grid, state; dt_min, dt_max, target_time, ...) changes $\Delta t$ from step to step:

  • Rejection. A step whose head solve does not converge, at $\Delta t > \Delta t_\min$, is retried from its start at half the step (no less than $\Delta t_\min$). Only the head needs resetting: the gap, the water and the head history change only once a step is accepted.
  • Growth. After grow_after (3) accepted steps in a row that each converged within fast_iters (30) Picard iterations, the step grows by grow (1.5), up to $\Delta t_\max$.
  • Rate cap. With safety_factor set, $\Delta t \le \text{safety\_factor} / r$, where $r$ is the largest (or, with use_percentile = true, the percentile quantile) of the gap's relaxation rate $C\, l_c'(b) + \gamma$ over the grounded cells above $b_\min$.
  • Landing on the target. The last step is cut to end exactly at target_time, and the one before it is shortened when the remainder would otherwise be less than $\Delta t_\min$.

Freeze-on capacity

With limit_freeze_on the melt rate is floored at minus the freeze-on that the next gap update can absorb without the gap falling below $b_\min$ (Melt on the grid). Each term is evaluated where the gap scheme evaluates it: opening by sliding and creep at $b_\min$ for FullyImplicitGapScheme, at $b^n$ for ExplicitGapScheme, and for WithDiffusion from the local terms of its gap system, ignoring the diffusion (which only moves gap volume between cells). When a host ice model asks for the capacity over its own, longer step (freeze_on_capacity!(C, sim; dt)), the room in the gap is spread over that step, and creep and sliding are evaluated at the current gap, since over many Shakti steps they act on the actual gap, not on $b_\min$. Under UnfilledCavities and on the dry floor the capacity is also capped by the water present, $(\rho_w/\rho_i)\, W / \Delta t$.

Mask changes

A coupled ice model can change which cells are solved, between steps:

  • freeze_cells! turns grounded cells into FROZEN_BED: $b = W = 0$, $p_w = 0$, $h = z_b$, and so $N = p_o$. The water they held is discarded, not refrozen.
  • thaw_cells! turns FROZEN_BED cells back into grounded ones, at $b = b_\min$ with no water (an empty cavity, or a dry floor), keeping their head as the start of the next solve.
  • update_frozen_mask! freezes grounded cells whose basal temperature relative to the pressure melting point is below T_freeze and thaws frozen cells at or above T_freeze + T_hysteresis.
  • set_mask! takes the host's mask, keeping frozen cells frozen. A cell that leaves the grounded set loses its gap and water and takes the boundary pressure of its new category; a cell that joins it gets $b = b_\min$ with no water, and its head starts from the boundary pressure it had.

Each refreshes the face masks, $N$, $\beta$ and $l_c$, and reports the water it discarded. update_frozen_mask! and set_mask! then freeze any grounded cell left with no neighbour that is grounded, LAND or OCEAN, since its water has nowhere to go and its row could be singular. The Simulation methods of these functions also bring the water within what the sheet allows, and refresh the fields that depend on the simulation's options (cell $N$ clamps, outflow-only faces).

Linear solvers

The head matrix is a five-point stencil. It is symmetric and positive definite with every face scheme except "implicit_upwind", whose Newton terms make it non-symmetric (it keeps the sign pattern of an M-matrix). The solvers:

SolverMatrix it solvesRepresentationFields on
LUDirectSolveranyassembledCPU
CholeskyDirectSolversymmetricassembledCPU
CUDSSDirectSolverany (structure = :general, LU) or symmetric (:spd, Cholesky)assembled on the CPU, factorized on an NVIDIA GPUCPU
CGIterativeSolver(grid, SparseAssembledLinearSystem)symmetricassembledCPU
CGIterativeSolver(grid, MatrixFreeLinearSystem)symmetricmatrix-freeCPU or GPU
BiCGStabIterativeSolveranymatrix-freeCPU or GPU

An assembled solver builds the sparse matrix explicitly, which only the CPU can do, so its fields stay on the CPU. A matrix-free solver never forms the matrix: it applies the five-point stencil from the per-cell coefficients, so it runs wherever the fields live, and it is the only kind that runs with the fields on a GPU.

The assembled matrix's sparsity pattern is built once, so each iterate only rewrites its values. The iterative solvers warm-start from the current head. A matrix-free solver is preconditioned by an aggregation-multigrid V-cycle by default (or Jacobi; CG also takes Chebyshev), and CG on the assembled matrix by algebraic multigrid. LUDirectSolver(grid; lagged = true) reuses its last factorization as the preconditioner of a GMRES solve and refactorizes only when GMRES stalls. Under WithDiffusion the gap system is symmetric positive definite, and takes its own solver (Cholesky, cuDSS with :spd, or CG). See the tutorial for when to use which.

Diagnostic fields

Some fields are computed for output only and never read by the solve: the cell-centred conductance $K = g b^3 / (12 \nu (1 + \omega \mathrm{Re}))$, the cell Reynolds number, the mean of its four faces, and the face gaps b_x, b_y, the mean of the two cells (the nearest cell's gap on the domain edge). The solve reads the face conductances K_x, K_y built from the water depth.
