Model equations

This page describes the continuum model solved by Shakti.jl. The sheet is Shakti's (Sommers et al., 2018), extended to cavities that are partly empty and to ice that is lifted off the bed, as in GlaDS-2 (Wells et al., 2026) and (Schoof et al., 2012). Each extension can be turned off (see the tutorial), and the equations then reduce to those of the original Shakti. The Discretization page shows how the equations are solved on the grid.

Unknowns and geometry

The model is two-dimensional in the horizontal coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x, y)$. Between the bed $z_b(\mathbf{x})$ and the ice base there is a gap of height $b(\mathbf{x}, t)$, which holds a depth $W(\mathbf{x}, t)$ of water volume per unit bed area. The unknowns are

SymbolMeaningUnitState field
$b$gap height (space between the bed and the ice base)mb
$h$reduced head, $h = z_b + (W - b) + p_w / (\rho_w g)$mh

and the derived fields are the water depth $W$ (water volume per unit bed area, stored in the State field W), given by $b$ and $h$ through the closure below, and

\[H = \max\!\left(0,\; z_s - z_b - b\right), \qquad p_o = \rho_i g H, \qquad N = p_o - p_w ,\]

where $z_s$ is the ice surface, $H$ the ice thickness above the gap, $p_o$ the overburden pressure, $p_w$ the water pressure, $N$ the effective pressure and $(x)_+ = \max(x, 0)$. Water above the gap in the uplifted regime lifts the ice without thinning it, so it does not enter $H$ and leaves $p_o$ unchanged. It will be useful to also express the overburden as a reduced head, $h_o = z_b + p_o / (\rho_w g)$.

Three regimes of the sheet

The reduced head and the water pressure are related by

\[p_w = \rho_w g \,\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(h - z_b,\; 0,\; \frac{p_o}{\rho_w g}\right), \qquad W = b + (h - z_b) - \frac{p_w}{\rho_w g} .\]

This one closure covers three regimes:

RegimeReduced headPressureWater
unfilled (cavity partly empty)$h < z_b$$p_w = 0$, $N = p_o$$W = b - (z_b - h) < b$
filled$z_b \le h \le h_o$$p_w = \rho_w g (h - z_b)$$W = b$
uplifted (ice lifted off the bed)$h > h_o$$p_w = p_o$, $N = 0$$W = b + (h - h_o) > b$

So the bounds $0 \le p_w \le p_o$ hold, and once $p_w$ reaches a bound, changes in $h$ are changes in the water depth. FilledCavities() removes the lower bound and NoUplift() the upper one. With both, Shakti's sheet is recovered: $W = b$ and $p_w = \rho_w g (h - z_b)$ without bounds, so $N < 0$ (water above the overburden) and $p_w < 0$ (suction) are both possible.

Water flux

The water moves as a thin viscous layer driven by the gradient of the hydraulic head $\hat{h}$, which is related to the reduced head by

\[\hat{h} = h + b \quad (\texttt{SheetTopPotential}), \qquad \hat{h} = h \quad (\texttt{BedPotential}).\]

With SheetTopPotential, $\hat{h} = z_b + W + p_w / (\rho_w g)$ is the height the water would reach in a borehole drilled to the bed (filled and uplifted regimes) or the elevation of the water surface (unfilled regime): in the unfilled regime $\hat{h} = z_b + W$ and in the uplifted regime $\hat{h} = h_o + W$. Multiplied by $\rho_w g$ it is the hydraulic potential, the energy density of the water, and the water flows to decrease it. The $+ W$ term accounts for the thickness of the water layer, as in (Wells et al., 2026) Eq. 7. BedPotential leaves it out, $\hat{h} = h$, as in (Sommers et al., 2018). The flux per unit width is

\[\mathbf{q} = -K \nabla \hat{h}, \qquad K = \frac{g\, W^3}{12 \nu \left(1 + \omega \,\mathrm{Re}\right)}, \qquad \mathrm{Re} = \frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{\nu} .\]

At small Reynolds numbers this is laminar Poiseuille flow between parallel plates. As $\mathrm{Re}$ grows, the factor $1 + \omega \mathrm{Re}$ takes the flux to the turbulent limit $|\mathbf{q}| \propto |\nabla\hat{h}|^{1/2}$. So the same equations describe distributed and channelized drainage, without a separate channel network (Sommers et al., 2018). The transmissivity is built from the water depth $W$, which equals $b$ in a filled gap, so an empty cavity carries no water. Since $\mathrm{Re}$ depends on $\mathbf{q}$, the two relations above make an implicit equation for $|\mathbf{q}|$. Writing $D = g W^3 |\nabla\hat{h}| / (12 \nu^2)$, the implicit equation is the quadratic equation $\omega \mathrm{Re}^2 + \mathrm{Re} - D = 0$, whose positive root is

\[\mathrm{Re} = \frac{2 D}{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 \omega D}} .\]

Water mass balance

The water volume per unit bed area changes by divergence of the flux, melt and input from above:

\[\frac{\partial W}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial b_e}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{q} = \frac{\dot m}{\rho_w} + i_{e\to b} .\]

$\dot m$ [kg m⁻² s⁻¹] is the melt rate of the ice at the base and $i_{e\to b}$ [m s⁻¹] the water that enters the bed from above (surface melt through moulins and crevasses, ieb). $b_e$ is the water stored in the ice above the bed, the englacial storage of (Sommers et al., 2018):

\[b_e = e_v \frac{p_w}{\rho_w g} = e_v \left( h - z_b - (W - b) \right),\]

with the englacial void ratio $e_v$ (e_v = 0 by default). The second form follows from the definition of $h$. The closure makes $W - b$ a function of $h$ in each regime, so $b_e$ is a function of $h$ and $p_o$:

Regime$W - b$$b_e$$\partial b_e / \partial t$
unfilled$h - z_b$$0$$0$
filled$0$$e_v (h - z_b)$$e_v\, \partial h / \partial t$
uplifted$h - h_o$$e_v\, p_o / (\rho_w g)$$e_v\, \partial p_o / \partial t \,/\, (\rho_w g)$

that is, $b_e = e_v \operatorname{clamp}(h - z_b,\, 0,\, p_o/(\rho_w g))$. Outside the filled range a change in head is a change in $W$, not in $b_e$, so the same water is not counted twice. In an uplifted cell $b_e$ still changes when the overburden does, since $p_o$ depends on $b$ and $z_s$ through $H$: water drains from the ice to the bed as $p_o$ falls. On Shakti's sheet (FilledCavities(), NoUplift()) there is no clamp and $b_e = e_v (h - z_b)$.

Gap height

The gap opens by melting of the ice roof and by sliding over bed bumps, and it closes by viscous creep of the ice:

\[\frac{\partial b}{\partial t} = \frac{\dot m}{\rho_i} + \beta(b)\, |\mathbf{u}_b| - A |N|^{n-1} N \, l_c(b) \;\left[ + \nabla \cdot (\mathcal{D} \nabla b) \right] .\]

$\mathbf{u}_b$ is the sliding velocity of the ice at its base, $A$ the ice softness (A_visc) and $n$ the exponent of Glen's law. The opening by sliding is that of a cavity in the lee of bumps of height $b_r$ and spacing $l_r$,

\[\beta(b) = \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{b_r^* - b}{l_r}\right), \qquad b_r^* = \max\!\left(0,\; b_r - (W - b)_+\right),\]

where $b_r^*$ is the height of the bumps still in contact with the ice: ice lifted by a layer $W - b$ rides over the bumps lower than that layer. Without uplift $b_r^* = b_r$. The creep length scale is $l_c = b$. With the cutoff of (Felden et al., 2023) Eq. 7 (b_c > 0) it is $l_c = b^2 / b_c$ for $b \le b_c$ and $N > 0$, which slows the closure of thin gaps. At $N < 0$ the creep term opens the gap.

The term in brackets is optional (WithDiffusion). It is the melting of channel walls by the heat dissipated in the channel, as in Suhmo (Felden et al., 2023) Eq. 10, with

\[\mathcal{D} = \frac{\min(W, b)}{\rho_i L} \left( Q_\mathrm{diss} + Q_\mathrm{sens} \right)_+ ,\]

where $Q_\mathrm{diss}$ and $Q_\mathrm{sens}$ are the two flow terms of the melt rate below.

Melt rate

The heat available at the bed melts the ice at the rate

\[L \dot m = G + \underbrace{|\mathbf{u}_b \cdot \boldsymbol{\tau}_b|}_{Q_b} \underbrace{- \rho_w g\, \mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla \hat{h}}_{Q_\mathrm{diss}} + \underbrace{c_t c_w \rho_w\, \mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla p_w}_{Q_\mathrm{sens}} - q_T ,\]

with the geothermal flux $G$, the frictional heat $Q_b$ of sliding with basal shear stress $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$, the heat $Q_\mathrm{diss} = \rho_w g K |\nabla\hat{h}|^2 \ge 0$ dissipated by the flow, the sensible heat $Q_\mathrm{sens}$ needed to keep the water at the pressure-melting point (which falls by $c_t$ per Pascal), and the heat $q_T$ conducted into the ice. We assume that water is always at its pressure-melting point temperature, so that water that flows towards lower pressure, $\mathbf{q} \cdot \nabla p_w < 0$, is warmed to its rising melting point, which takes heat from melting. Each term can be turned off (mdot_includes_* in ModelParameters). $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b$ comes from a sliding law in $N$ and $\mathbf{u}_b$ (table in the tutorial) or from an ice-flow model. Freeze-on ($\dot m < 0$) is limited to the water the gap holds.

Head equation

Subtracting the gap equation from the mass balance eliminates the time derivative of the gap and gives an equation for the head. In a filled cell ($W = b$) it reads

\[-\nabla \cdot \left( K \nabla (h + b) \right) + e_v \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} = \dot m \left( \frac{1}{\rho_w} - \frac{1}{\rho_i} \right) - \beta |\mathbf{u}_b| + A |N|^{n-1} N \, l_c \left[ - \nabla \cdot (\mathcal{D} \nabla b) \right] + i_{e\to b} ,\]

with $N = p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)$. The left side is the net outflow of water plus its englacial storage. The right side is the net source of water to the cell once the change in gap volume has been subtracted. Its terms are:

  • melt: the water $\dot m / \rho_w$ produced minus the gap volume $\dot m / \rho_i$ opened. This is negative, since $\rho_w > \rho_i$: the gap grows by more than the water it receives,
  • sliding: $-\beta |\mathbf{u}_b|$, since the cavities that sliding opens add gap volume but no water,
  • creep: $+A |N|^{n-1} N\, l_c$, the water pushed out as the ice closes the gap,
  • the input $i_{e\to b}$.

With $e_v = 0$ the equation is elliptic in $h$ at each time. It is nonlinear because $N$ enters the creep term, and because $\mathrm{Re}$ and $\dot m$ enter $K$ and the right side. With $\hat{h} = h$ it is the head equation of (Sommers et al., 2018).

In an unfilled or uplifted cell the water depth and the gap height differ, $W \ne b$, and the equation changes in two ways.

First, $N$ is fixed (at $p_o$ in an unfilled cell, at $0$ in an uplifted one), so the creep term no longer depends on $h$.

Second, a storage term appears on the left. The water depth changes in time as $\partial W / \partial t = \partial b / \partial t + \partial (W - b) / \partial t$. The gap equation gives $\partial b / \partial t$, and that part is what the equation above already contains. The second part, $\partial (W - b) / \partial t$, is zero in a filled cell because $W - b = 0$. It is not zero here, and the closure ties it to the head: $W - b = h - z_b$ in an unfilled cell and $W - b = h - h_o$ in an uplifted one. So a change in $W - b$ is a change in $h$, and the term is a storage term $\partial h / \partial t$ on the left, like the englacial one but with coefficient 1 instead of $e_v$. The englacial term itself no longer depends on $h$ there (see Water mass balance): it is zero in an unfilled cell and $e_v\, \partial p_o / \partial t \,/\, (\rho_w g)$, a source of water, in an uplifted one.

The head equation and the gap equation depend on each other's unknown, so they cannot be solved one after the other. The head equation needs the gap height $b$, which sets the conductivity $K$. The gap equation needs the effective pressure $N$, which is set by the head $h$ through $N = p_o - \rho_w g (h - z_b)$. Discretization shows how a time step solves them together.

Boundary conditions

Every cell has a mask value. The equations above hold on GROUNDED cells. The other values set the boundary conditions:

MaskCondition
OCEAN$h = z_b + \left(p_\mathrm{atm} - \rho_{sw} g \min(z_b, 0)\right) / (\rho_w g)$: the pressure of the ocean at the bed
LAND$h = z_b + p_\mathrm{atm} / (\rho_w g)$: water leaves at atmospheric pressure
OTHER_BASINno flux across the face, $\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0$, i.e. $\partial \hat{h} / \partial n = 0$; the cell is not solved, so its head $h$ stays at its initial value for the whole run, which is the one of LAND, $h = z_b + p_\mathrm{atm} / (\rho_w g)$
FROZEN_BEDno flux across the face, $\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0$, i.e. $\partial \hat{h} / \partial n = 0$; the cell is not solved and holds no water: $b = 0$, $W = 0$, $p_w = 0$, so $h = z_b$ and $N = p_o$
edge of the gridno flux, $\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0$, i.e. $\partial \hat{h} / \partial n = 0$

By default OCEAN and LAND faces are outflow only (outflow_only_land, outflow_only_ocean): the face closes when the boundary head is above the hydraulic head $\hat{h}$ of the grounded cell, so water can only flow out of the grounded cell into the ocean or land, never from the boundary into the grounded cell.
